# 05 — History Timeline

Dates use **BC** for Before Concord and **AC** for After Concord. Official dates are reliable only after the Aurelith consolidation.

## 1. The Falling of the Pale Moon — mythic prehistory

- **What happened:** a moon, god-bone, or celestial archive fell into Edrath, seeding Neodym through mountain roots and sea trenches.
- **Official version:** the First Stillness blessed the world with memory.
- **Forbidden version:** a cold shackle fell to bind the Laughing Flame.
- **Consequences:** Neodym veins, strange magic, and the future Aurelith emergence.
- **Story hooks:** a second moon fragment may lie under the Unnumbered Sea.

## 2. The Root Age — unknown

- **What happened:** Thallor root-minds spread across wet forests and stored living memory before written peoples.
- **Official version:** pre-civilized ecology, not history.
- **Forbidden version:** the Thallor remember the world before Aurelith self-myth.
- **Consequences:** Morrowfen remains resistant to prediction.
- **Story hooks:** a Thallor elder remembers a human name attached to the first Neodym rite.

## 3. The First Human Hearths — c. 4100 BC

- **What happened:** early human city-states rose around rivers, cattle roads, and fire temples.
- **Official version:** passionate but unstable tribes needed guidance.
- **Forbidden version:** humans built rich republics, libraries, and star shrines before Aurelith rule.
- **Consequences:** human memory becomes politically contested.
- **Story hooks:** buried hearth tablets contradict school histories.

## 4. The Awakening of Neodym — c. 2600 BC

- **What happened:** humans, Cairnkin, and Dravyr learned to refine moon-silver ore.
- **Official version:** reckless mortals disturbed holy matter.
- **Forbidden version:** shared discovery was later claimed by the Aurelith.
- **Consequences:** new magic, new weapons, predictive rites.
- **Story hooks:** a Cairnkin tool bears the oldest known Neodym inscription.

## 5. The Sky Fever — c. 2300 BC

- **What happened:** a mass ritual or war triggered weather madness, spirit-plague, and memory storms.
- **Official version:** human chaos nearly ended the world.
- **Forbidden version:** Aurelith intervention, not human action alone, worsened the disaster.
- **Consequences:** millions died; the argument for immortal governance was born.
- **Story hooks:** surviving Eirrem may be victims of emergency soul trials.

## 6. The Arrival of the Aurelith — c. 2210 BC

- **What happened:** the Aurelith emerged from sealed sanctuaries, moon craters, or clarified human lineages.
- **Official version:** they descended as appointed stewards.
- **Forbidden version:** they were created during The Sky Fever as living instruments of control.
- **Consequences:** guidance begins.
- **Story hooks:** the first Aurelith council included a mortal woman whose name was erased.

## 7. The Age of Guidance — 2100 to 800 BC

- **What happened:** Aurelith healers, engineers, and teachers helped rebuild Edrath.
- **Official version:** benevolent restoration.
- **Forbidden version:** gratitude became dependency by design.
- **Consequences:** roads, aqueducts, archives, crop wards, and human trust.
- **Story hooks:** old treaties promised eventual human self-rule.

## 8. The Mercy of Chains — 799 BC

- **What happened:** after renewed human wars, the Quiet Concord declared permanent custodianship.
- **Official version:** emergency peace decree.
- **Forbidden version:** a bloodless coup using famine relief and memory law.
- **Consequences:** human sovereignty became provincial privilege.
- **Story hooks:** the original decree includes an expiration clause hidden in the First Memory archive.

## 9. The Bread Betrayal — 612 BC

- **What happened:** human collaborator houses helped identify rebel granaries in exchange for sealed titles.
- **Official version:** prevention of famine terrorism.
- **Forbidden version:** collaborators starved rival cities and blamed rebels.
- **Consequences:** Sealed Houses gained power; rebel distrust of nobles hardened.
- **Story hooks:** a modern collaborator descends from both betrayers and victims.

## 10. The Dravyr Binding — 440 BC

- **What happened:** surviving dragonlike Dravyr were defeated and their heartfire bound in sanctuaries.
- **Official version:** monsters pacified for public safety.
- **Forbidden version:** a sovereign race was reduced to sacred fuel.
- **Consequences:** refined Neodym infrastructure expanded rapidly.
- **Story hooks:** freeing one heart could collapse an archive city.

## 11. The White Census — 0 AC

- **What happened:** the first continent-wide identity seals were imposed.
- **Official version:** universal protection and fair distribution.
- **Forbidden version:** the birth of total custodianship.
- **Consequences:** the After Concord calendar begins.
- **Story hooks:** unsealed descendants of pre-census families can move unseen.

## 12. The Red Choir Rebellion — 318 AC

- **What happened:** Bloodsong rebels briefly seized three provinces.
- **Official version:** cult uprising stopped by mercy.
- **Forbidden version:** a popular liberation movement was betrayed from within.
- **Consequences:** Bloodsong bans, Measure Days, expanded Mirror Choir.
- **Story hooks:** the final rebel song was never captured.

## 13. The Blank Kilns — 487 AC

- **What happened:** an immortality experiment or rebel attack burned a city out of memory, creating the Eirrem.
- **Official version:** human extremists destroyed a mercy facility.
- **Forbidden version:** Aurelith soul experiments failed.
- **Consequences:** ghost zones, stricter archive secrecy.
- **Story hooks:** Eirrem testimony can expose the Unlit Seat.

## 14. The Quiet Plague — 702 AC

- **What happened:** a plague of emotional numbness spread through Aurelith high houses.
- **Official version:** contained spiritual illness.
- **Forbidden version:** immortality itself is failing.
- **Consequences:** increased extraction of human memories for archive vitality.
- **Story hooks:** Aurelith need human feeling to survive.

## 15. The Emberwake Begins — 871 AC

- **What happened:** scattered rebel cells unified around a forbidden funeral song.
- **Official version:** criminal conspiracy.
- **Forbidden version:** the first broad alliance of humans, Cairnkin, Nacren, Thallor, and dissident Aurelith.
- **Consequences:** present resistance network.
- **Story hooks:** the song's composer may still be alive inside a Moon Vault.

## 16. Present crisis: The Wrong Prophecy — 913 AC

- **What happened:** an Orrery Court predicts a massacre that does not occur, while an unpredicted mine collapse kills thousands.
- **Official version:** localized instrument error.
- **Forbidden version:** the Mirror Choir is going blind.
- **Consequences:** hardliners demand tighter control; reformers see proof of failure; rebels sense an opening.
- **Story hooks:** the error centers on one human child, one dissident Aurelith, and one missing memory.

## Visual notes

- History should feel layered: polished official reliefs covering scratched human murals, archive plaques over older names, ruins repurposed as schools.
- Every historical image should include competing evidence: one official symbol and one forbidden clue.

## Open questions

- Which events are outright lies versus selective truths?
- What did the expiration clause of the Mercy of Chains say?
- Did The Wrong Prophecy happen because of human magic, Aurelith decay, or Neodym awakening?
- Who benefits most if the truth of The Sky Fever remains hidden?
