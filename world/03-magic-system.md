# 03 - Magic and wonder

## Principle

Magic in Neodym should feel older than explanation and smaller than a system. Human characters should know practices, taboos, prayers, tricks, and consequences. They should not speak like scholars explaining a solved mechanism.

## Human magic

Human magic is uneven, local, risky, and often deniable.

Forms:

- oath songs
- saint candles
- funeral verses
- blood promises
- harvest charms
- boundary bread
- road blessings
- plague knots
- name taboos
- weather prayers
- witness marks cut into wood or stone

Most human magic should cost something social, spiritual, bodily, or legal. Much of it may be superstition until the scene forces the reader to wonder.

## Aurelith craft

Aurelith works are not a curriculum for humans. They appear as impossible high fantasy:

- stone that ignores weather
- light without flame
- towers that defeat distance
- boundaries no map draws the same way twice
- roads horses refuse
- relics that make human oaths feel suddenly too small

Do not explain the craft. Do not give it modern language.

## Neodym

Neodym may be involved in Aurelith works and rare relics. Humans do not mine it safely or use it casually. Names for it vary by region: moonbone, white stone, saint-metal, witch-silver, elder ore.

## What to avoid

- academies that turn human magic into a neat ladder
- combat spell lists
- technical diagrams
- Aurelith instruction scenes
- universal identity marks or daily magical registration
- magic as the first answer to a human plot problem

## Open questions

- Which human rites are real, which are comfort, and which are fraud?
- What one small folk practice matters in Book One without becoming the plot's engine?
- Which later saga story deserves true Aurelith artifact contact?
