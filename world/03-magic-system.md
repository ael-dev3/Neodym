# 03 — Magic System

## Name and source

Magic in Edrath is called **the Mnemonic Loom** by scholars, though most people use local names: craft, song, oathwork, mirrorwork, root-speech, salt-dreaming, or bloodfire. It arises from the tension between memory, desire, matter, and mortality. The world remembers being shaped, and living beings can persuade those memories to move.

Neodym is the strongest known memory-bearing material. It can hold impressions of touch, speech, vow, fear, death, and possible motion. This makes it the foundation of Aurelith power and the most dangerous substance in rebel hands.

## How magic is practiced

Magic requires three components:

1. **Pattern:** a song, gesture, diagram, calculation, dance, ritual scar, or architectural form.
2. **Witness:** a mind, spirit, community, archive, ancestor, god, or object that remembers the pattern.
3. **Cost:** fatigue, blood, lost memory, shortened life, public obligation, emotional exposure, or spiritual debt.

The more witnesses a spell has, the more stable it becomes. The more personal the cost, the harder it is to predict.

## Who can use it

Almost anyone can perform small household craft: luck knots, hearth wards, bread blessings, weather prayers. More significant work requires training, inheritance, trauma, devotion, or exposure to Neodym.

- Aurelith excel at stable, repeatable, large-scale workings.
- Humans excel at volatile, intimate, improvisational workings.
- Cairnkin shape stone memory and ore breath.
- Thallor exchange ancestral memories through spores and decay.
- Vharakul use oath scars and battle trance.
- Nacren shape salt dreams, tides, and moon-reflection.
- Caltrow Kith break pattern locks and confuse foretold paths.

## Costs and limitations

Magic cannot create perfect knowledge, true resurrection, costless healing, or permanent peace. Every memory stored can be stolen. Every prediction excludes something. Every archive decays in ways its keepers refuse to see.

Common costs:

- **Memory pallor:** losing emotional color from a stored memory.
- **Oath weight:** becoming physically burdened by broken vows.
- **Name thinning:** identity weakening after too much sealwork.
- **Bright fever:** Neodym poisoning that produces visions and tumors.
- **Grief burn:** human Bloodsong shortening the singer's life.

## Aurelith magic: silver law

Aurelith magic is precise, architectural, and civic. It favors:

- Fate arithmetic in Orrery Courts.
- Mirror surveillance through still water and polished Neodym.
- Memory extraction and storage in Moon Vaults.
- Identity seals that bind name, face, lineage, debt, and permitted travel.
- Large public works: aqueducts, floating bridges, climate veils, plague wards.
- Pacification rites that lower the chance of riot or battle.

Its weakness is overconfidence. It struggles when people act from love, shame, religious ecstasy, or grief in ways that do not optimize survival.

## Human magic: Bloodsong

Forbidden human magic is called **Bloodsong** by rebels and **Mortal Distortion** by Aurelith courts. It uses breath, heartbeat, ancestry, pain, and chosen risk. Bloodsong is strongest at thresholds: birth, death, wedding, execution, exile, first love, last stand.

Bloodsong can:

- Hide a message inside melody beyond mirror-reading.
- Make a vow burn through an identity seal.
- Awaken dead memories in objects.
- Break a predicted sequence by binding people to an impossible promise.
- Call ancestral presences for counsel or rage.
- Heal through shared pain, at real cost.

Bloodsong threatens the Concord because it proves mortality is not merely a weakness. Death gives human acts a weight immortal planning cannot fully simulate.

## Other magical traditions

- **Cairnkin stone-breath:** slow shaping of tunnels, ore, and pressure; dangerous around raw Neodym.
- **Thallor root-remembrance:** communal memory, truth rot, healing fungi, forbidden historical visions.
- **Vharakul oath-trance:** courage, fear-scent, wound endurance, and battle judgement.
- **Dravyr heartfire:** metal awakening, weather memory, volcanic transformation.
- **Eirrem ash-echo:** haunting, testimony, memory possession, white flame.
- **Nacren tide-dreaming:** concealment at sea, moon navigation, dream messages that dissolve on waking.
- **Caltrow misrule:** charms that tangle probability, especially useful against sealed doors and predicted patrols.

## Forbidden magic

- **Soul indexing:** binding a living soul into a ledger without consent.
- **Clarification theft:** using human children as raw identity material for Aurelith reproduction.
- **Dead prophecy:** calculating futures from murdered memories.
- **Name erasure:** removing a person from public recognition.
- **Fire of the Laughing Wound:** a human rite rumored to unmake Neodym bindings.

## Memory as weapon

Memories can be stored, edited, traded, taxed, inherited, or used as evidence. A stolen memory may leave the victim with facts but no feeling. A forged memory can convict an innocent person. A curated family archive can make collaborators believe they were always loyal.

## Prophecy and uncertainty

Prophecy in Edrath is calculated from memory, motion, omen, and desire. It can be astonishingly accurate, but never perfect. The more a prediction is used to govern people, the more it changes the people being predicted. Emotions disrupt prophecy not because they are random, but because they make values visible that survival mathematics cannot rank.

## Magic in society

Magic shapes class and law. Those with stable seal-compatible gifts become clerks, engineers, healers, or court singers. Those with volatile gifts are restricted, conscripted, sterilized by law in hardline provinces, or smuggled into rebel cells. Magical education is the heart of political control.

## Visual notes

- Aurelith magic: thin silver lines, geometric halos, floating script, black mirrors, cold moonlight.
- Human Bloodsong: red thread, breath vapor, candle flame bending toward voices, skin-lit scars, imperfect circles.
- Neodym effects: magnetic dust, blue-silver glow, levitating filings, memory images trapped like insects in crystal.

## Open questions

- Does Neodym remember possible futures or only past patterns projected forward?
- Can Bloodsong heal the damage caused by memory theft?
- What is the final cost of breaking the Mirror Choir?
- Is there a form of magic only possible when Aurelith and humans act together freely?
