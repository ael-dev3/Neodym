# 00 - High concept

## Core idea

Edrath is a human high-fantasy world that happens to share its horizon with something older.

Humans build kingdoms, fight border wars, raise saints, crown bad kings, betray brothers, write songs, starve villages, make peace, break peace, and blame the gods when their own greed catches fire. Human life is not a schoolroom run by immortals. It is human: brave, stupid, holy, cruel, funny, hungry, and short.

The Aurelith are an ancient elf-like civilization bound to the rare substance called Neodym. They are vastly more advanced than human kingdoms, but they are not daily rulers of human life. They are distant, rare, and difficult to interpret. Most humans know them through rumor, taboo, ruins, noble lies, priestly arguments, forbidden roads, and the occasional light on a hill that nobody can explain.

## Dramatic question

How do humans live, love, fight, and lie in a world whose edges are shaped by powers they cannot negotiate with?

## The thin veil

The Aurelith layer should feel like a veil over the world rather than a hand around every throat. It is there in boundaries, old treaties, impossible structures, and elite fear. It is not there in every tax roll, every classroom, every village court, or every song.

Most stories should begin with human pressure and stay there.

## Human agency

Human problems usually have human authors:

- lords raise levies for greedy wars
- priests turn uncertainty into doctrine
- nobles claim ancient favor to justify rank
- merchants starve towns for profit
- families sell daughters and sons into bargains
- border peoples are blamed for disasters they did not cause
- kings use old Aurelith silence as political theater

The Aurelith may shape terrain, law, religion, or fear at the edge of a conflict, but they should not be the simple answer to why people suffer.

## Aurelith presence

Use the Aurelith through surface effects:

- a road no horse will step onto
- a tower seen from every direction but never reached
- a field where banners are lowered by old custom
- a noble vault containing a relic nobody can use
- a treaty phrase older than the language around it
- a light that appears over a hill once a year
- a silence from an envoy that changes a war

Do not explain how these work. Human explanations should disagree.

## Neodym

Neodym is rare, old, and mostly outside ordinary human craft. Humans may call it moonbone, white stone, witch-silver, saint-metal, or nothing at all depending on region. It appears in Aurelith works, ancient boundaries, and a few dangerous relics. It is not a normal village technology.

## Contact levels

Level 0: no contact. Most humans never see an Aurelith.

Level 1: mythic background. Songs, taboos, sermons, noble claims, contradictory rumors.

Level 2: environmental presence. Forbidden hills, silent towers, roadless territories, old boundaries.

Level 3: artifact contact. A human encounters something Aurelith-made and probably misunderstands it.

Level 4: diplomatic contact. Rare envoys to kings, high priests, ancient houses, or treaty courts.

Level 5: direct intervention. Extremely rare. It should feel like disaster, miracle, or angelic visitation.

## Open questions

- Which human kingdoms matter most for the first larger arc after Book One?
- Which Aurelith boundaries are old treaties and which are simply places humans learned to avoid?
- How much of human religion comes from real encounters versus convenient lies?
