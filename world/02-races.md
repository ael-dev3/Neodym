# 02 — Races and Peoples

## The Aurelith

### Names

- **Self-name:** Aurelith, meaning roughly "those clarified by silver memory."
- **Human names:** Silverbloods, Quiet Ones, Perfected, Moon Judges, the Neodym.
- **Formal state name:** The Quiet Concord.

### Appearance

Aurelith are tall, slender, and unnervingly symmetrical. Their skin ranges from ivory to ash-pale umber with faint metallic undertones. Their veins shine blue-silver under moonlight. Their ears taper subtly, but they should not read as familiar forest elves. Their beauty is architectural: smooth, intentional, almost too resolved. Hair is worn long, braided with Neodym thread, often white, black, silver, or translucent blond. Eyes appear pale gray, mirrored violet, moon-blue, or black with silver flecks.

### Lifespan and reproduction

Aurelith do not reproduce freely. New Aurelith are created through **Clarification**, a rare rite in which chosen memory, Neodym dust, lineage essence, and a willing parent-song are shaped in crystal wombs called **Lunaries**. The process takes decades. Some Clarified children inherit memories they never lived, making childhood strange and solemn.

Aurelith can live for millennia, but immortality is not effortless. They require periodic communion with Neodym archives to stabilize memory and identity. Without it, they suffer **Drift**, a condition in which centuries blur and empathy thins into pattern-recognition.

### Abilities

- Read emotional residue from objects and rooms.
- Store selected memories in Neodym crystals.
- Trace probable consequences through fate arithmetic.
- Move with inhuman precision and silence.
- Shape silver, glass, and living stone through chant-geometry.
- Resist disease, age, and ordinary poisons.
- Maintain calm under terror.

### Limitations

- Poor at understanding sudden human improvisation.
- Emotionally cautious; powerful feelings can destabilize long-held predictions.
- Dependent on Neodym infrastructure for memory continuity.
- Slow reproduction makes them strategically conservative.
- Haunted by accumulated grief and boredom.
- Vulnerable to Bloodsong, mortal vows, and places where prediction weakens.

### Social structure

Aurelith society is arranged by function rather than family alone:

- **Mnemonic Houses:** preserve histories and lineage memories.
- **Orrery Courts:** calculate lawful futures and advise governance.
- **Mirror Orders:** maintain surveillance, census, and identity seals.
- **Lunary Choirs:** oversee Clarification and reproduction.
- **Ivory Legions:** enforce borders and suppress magical catastrophe.
- **Aesthetic Synods:** maintain beauty as civic discipline.

### Philosophy

Their central doctrine is **Continuance**: no living generation has the right to endanger the continuity of the world. Freedom is valuable only within the bounds of survivable futures. They believe humans confuse intensity with truth and choice with wisdom.

### Governance

The highest authority is **The Quiet Concord**, a council of nine immortal offices rather than nine fixed people. Each office is bound to a Neodym archive and can outlast its holder. The Concord rules through decrees called **Mercies**, each framed as protection.

### What they fear

- A second Sky Fever.
- Human magic that grows stronger through grief and death.
- The collapse of Neodym infrastructure.
- Their own emotional sterility.
- A prophecy proving that humanity must be free for the world to survive.

### What they secretly need from humans

Humans generate unrepeatable memories, songs, and emotional intensities that keep the archives from becoming sterile. Some Aurelith suspect Neodym itself feeds on mortal change. Others believe only humans can repair a flaw in the oldest prophecy engines because only mortal minds can make meaningful choices under true uncertainty.

### Beauty, emotion, and mortality

Aurelith treat beauty as civic law. A crooked street is not merely ugly; it is an ethical failure because disorder invites harm. Emotion is respected when refined into art, vow, or mourning rite, but feared when spontaneous. Mortality fascinates them. Some envy the moral clarity death gives to human love.

### Internal factions

1. **The Silver Preservers** — hardliners who believe human liberty must never again be allowed to threaten the world.
2. **The Veiled Reformers** — Aurelith who want gradual self-rule for human provinces under safeguards.
3. **The Orrery Hands** — prophecy engineers who trust fate arithmetic more than any living conscience.
4. **The Moon Archivists** — memory keepers obsessed with preserving every life, even by stealing memories at death.
5. **The Ivory Quiet** — military enforcers who suppress revolt, monsters, plague cults, and uncontrolled magic.
6. **The Mercy Heresy** — human-sympathizers who believe love requires risk.
7. **The Hollow Crown** — isolationists who want to abandon humanity and seal the archive cities.
8. **The Final Clarifiers** — extremists who want humanity transformed, merged, or erased to end historical chaos.

## Humans

### Legal and social status

Humans are subjects of protected provinces. They can marry, trade, worship approved saints, own limited land, travel under seal, and serve in guilds. They cannot maintain unregistered armies, teach uncensored history, practice Bloodsong publicly, forge Neodym, enter high archives, or rule without Aurelith oversight.

### Control methods

- **Name-Irons:** identity seals worn as bracelets, throat-rings, or stamped papers.
- **Census hymns:** public rituals that update births, deaths, marriages, crimes, and debts.
- **Mirror wells:** scrying basins in courts, schools, gates, and granaries.
- **Prophetic schooling:** children tested for dangerous gifts and redirected into permitted lives.
- **Bread ledgers:** food distribution tied to compliance.
- **Edited calendars:** historical anniversaries renamed or removed.

### Remaining freedoms

Humans retain household rites, local markets, most crafts, seasonal festivals, sanctioned theater, marriage choice in many regions, and village councils for minor disputes. These freedoms make the regime tolerable enough to endure.

### Why humans are dangerous

Human magic thrives on contradiction, grief, desire, and mortality. Humans can create new songs that have no precedent in the archives. They can make irrational sacrifices that break predicted outcomes. They can also become mobs, tyrants, cults, and war bands with terrifying speed.

### Collaborators and rebels

- **Sealed Houses:** privileged human families that administer provinces for the Concord.
- **The Emberwake:** decentralized rebels using songs, candle codes, smuggled memories, and old roads.
- **Gray guilds:** merchants who obey publicly and move contraband privately.
- **Bread loyalists:** common people who fear rebellion will starve their children.

### Human cultures

- **Hearthmarch:** farming clans with matriarchal hearth contracts and hidden saint songs.
- **Veyr Coast:** shipwright cities watched closely because the sea weakens prediction.
- **Rustvale:** mining towns where human and Cairnkin workers share bitter loyalties.
- **Glassmeadow:** wealthy collaborator vineyards and schools for sealed children.
- **Ash Chapels:** wandering religious communities preserving forbidden funerary rites.

### Family, art, and survival

Human families tend to be intergenerational and practical, with grandparents serving as living archives when written memory is unsafe. Human art is rhythmic, asymmetrical, and coded: quilt maps, lullaby warnings, bread stamps, pottery saints, funeral drums, and work songs with double meanings. Survival often means saying the approved words in public and teaching the dangerous ones at bedtime.

### Human moral flaws

Humans are not purely noble victims. Some rebel cells execute collaborators without trial. Some human lords dream of replacing the Concord with hereditary conquest. Some Bloodsong cults romanticize catastrophe. Some black-market memory traders exploit grief. Human freedom may unleash old sins along with new hope.

## Other races

### Cairnkin

- **Description:** short, dense-bodied subterranean people with basalt skin, amber eyes, iron-gray braids, and lichen tattoos that glow near Neodym.
- **Culture:** oath-miners, tunnel jurists, ancestor masons, and bitter humorists. They measure wealth in safe air and unbroken tools.
- **Biology/metaphysics:** their bones absorb mineral memory. Long exposure to Neodym causes prophetic tumors called brightstones.
- **Homeland:** the **Nyxvein Deeps** beneath Rustvale and the Hollow Moon Mines.
- **Neodym relationship:** indispensable miners, formally protected, functionally exploited.
- **Human relationship:** labor solidarity mixed with resentment because humans often supervise them for Aurelith wages.
- **Role:** mining, siegecraft, underworld navigation, forbidden ore trade.
- **Internal conflict:** accept safer Concord contracts or join mine rebellions that may poison their own children.
- **Story potential:** a Cairnkin collapse reveals an archive older than the Aurelith.
- **Visual identity:** basalt, ochre lichen, copper tools, respirator veils of woven fungus, angular stone shrines.

### Vharakul

- **Description:** tall beastlike border people with horned brows, maned necks, heavy claws, and hide patterned like storm clouds or dry grass.
- **Culture:** martial clans bound by adoption, trophy poetry, and oath scars.
- **Biology/metaphysics:** heightened smell for fear and spirit-taint; enter battle trances through drum-breath.
- **Homeland:** the **Gravewind Marches**, a borderland of tall grass, bone towers, and monster roads.
- **Neodym relationship:** recruited as auxiliary soldiers, paid in medicine and clan protections.
- **Human relationship:** feared by rebels, respected by frontier villages they defend.
- **Role:** border war, monster hunting, prison convoy guards, mercenary diplomacy.
- **Internal conflict:** serve the Concord for clan survival or break oaths to stop atrocities.
- **Story potential:** a Vharakul captain is ordered to burn a village that once saved his child.
- **Visual identity:** rawhide, black iron, bone beads, crimson oath scars, curved glaives, storm banners.

### Thallor

- **Description:** fungal-humanoid memory people with barklike masks, translucent caps, moss robes, and many small luminous eyes beneath hooded growths.
- **Culture:** communal, slow-speaking, ancestral, and ecological. They hold councils by exchanging spores of memory.
- **Biology/metaphysics:** individuals are temporary fruiting bodies of vast root-minds.
- **Homeland:** the **Morrowfen**, a drowned forest beyond clean Aurelith prediction.
- **Neodym relationship:** Neodym crystallizes their memory roots painfully; they avoid it but know its oldest secrets.
- **Human relationship:** shelter some rebels, distrust human fire and impatience.
- **Role:** memory, medicine, hidden history, poison gardens, sacred decomposition.
- **Internal conflict:** reveal ancient truth and risk forest destruction, or remain hidden.
- **Story potential:** their root-mind remembers a human face in the first Aurelith council.
- **Visual identity:** green-black wet bark, white spores, amber lamps, root bridges, mushroom script.

### Dravyr

- **Description:** dragonlike beings now rarely seen whole; many exist as chained hearts, fossil wings, or flame-souls housed in temple furnaces.
- **Culture:** once sky sovereigns of migration, riddle law, and volcanic song.
- **Biology/metaphysics:** living furnaces whose breath awakens metal and whose bones remember weather.
- **Homeland:** the **Cinder Crowns**, volcanic ranges now dotted with Aurelith extraction sanctuaries.
- **Neodym relationship:** their heartfire can refine Neodym. This made them sacred prisoners.
- **Human relationship:** old human epics remember them as tyrants and teachers.
- **Role:** energy, relics, imperial legitimacy, forbidden alliances.
- **Internal conflict:** surviving Dravyr disagree between vengeance, sleep, and bargaining.
- **Story potential:** a rebel must decide whether freeing a Dravyr will liberate a province or burn it.
- **Visual identity:** obsidian scales, ember cracks, gold-black horns, chain halos, furnace temples.

### Eirrem

- **Description:** pale ghostlike people with translucent skin, blurred edges, and voices that sound like remembered rooms.
- **Culture:** fragments of etiquette, grief, and lost identity gathered into wandering courts.
- **Biology/metaphysics:** created by failed Aurelith immortality experiments on human souls.
- **Homeland:** the **Blank Kilns**, ruins where memory burns white and maps forget themselves.
- **Neodym relationship:** Neodym holds them together and imprisons them.
- **Human relationship:** pity, terror, kinship; many were once human.
- **Role:** haunting, espionage, testimony, spiritual dread.
- **Internal conflict:** seek restoration, revenge, or release into true death.
- **Story potential:** an Eirrem witness knows which Aurelith office ordered the first soul trials.
- **Visual identity:** white ash, black funeral veils, silver cracks, floating wax seals, empty masks.

### Nacren

- **Description:** sea-adapted people with pearl-dark skin, finlike ear frills, flexible limbs, and bioluminescent scar patterns.
- **Culture:** tide courts, shell diplomacy, memory knots, and law sung in currents.
- **Biology/metaphysics:** their dreams move with the moon and cannot be held easily in Neodym.
- **Homeland:** the **Unnumbered Sea** and reef cities beneath storm glass.
- **Neodym relationship:** trade in small amounts but reject archive rule.
- **Human relationship:** smugglers, rescuers, raiders, and ambiguous allies.
- **Role:** sea routes, prophecy disruption, maritime sanctuary, salt magic.
- **Internal conflict:** profit from human rebellion or risk war against the Concord.
- **Story potential:** a Nacren envoy offers a route to a lost moon fragment below the sea.
- **Visual identity:** pearl, ink-blue, coral knives, kelp silk, shell masks, moonlit water maps.

### Caltrow Kith

- **Description:** small, quick, foxlike or goblinlike pattern-breakers with bright eyes, long fingers, and patchwork clothing covered in bells, knots, and stolen seals.
- **Culture:** trick contracts, impossible riddles, prank funerals, and sacred misdirection.
- **Biology/metaphysics:** they shed probability like fur; predictions slide off them.
- **Homeland:** everywhere borders overlap, especially **Jester Markets** under bridges and ruined gates.
- **Neodym relationship:** Neodym becomes erratic near them, making the Concord both fascinated and furious.
- **Human relationship:** beloved smugglers, unreliable allies, legendary cheats.
- **Role:** black markets, message-breaking, anti-prophecy charms, comic danger.
- **Internal conflict:** mischief for survival versus responsibility when jokes get people killed.
- **Story potential:** a Caltrow child accidentally makes an entire archive forget one rebel name.
- **Visual identity:** rust-red cloth, cracked bells, mismatched masks, crooked daggers, chalk sigils.

## Visual notes

- Each race should carry a material logic: silver/crystal for Aurelith, cloth/wood/clay for humans, basalt/lichen for Cairnkin, hide/bone for Vharakul, fungus/root for Thallor, obsidian/ember for Dravyr, ash/wax for Eirrem, pearl/salt for Nacren, bells/patchwork for Caltrow Kith.
- Avoid default fantasy silhouettes. Make every people recognizable from clothing, posture, architecture, and material culture.

## Open questions

- Are Aurelith and humans related through a hidden origin?
- Can Eirrem become fully alive again?
- Did Dravyr help create or awaken Neodym?
- Which other race holds the most reliable account of The Sky Fever?
