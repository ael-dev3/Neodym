# 02 - Peoples of Edrath

## Humans

Humans are the main historical force in most human lands. They are not a single victim class. They are farmers, kings, saints, raiders, judges, fools, lovers, tyrants, deserters, healers, poets, and murderers.

Human cultures vary by kingdom, region, faith, class, accent, and history. A peasant in the Wickerlow Hearthmarch may know nothing about Aurelith envoys but know every sheep sickness by smell.

## Aurelith

The Aurelith are ancient, elf-like, long-lived, and bound in some unclear way to Neodym. They are not human rulers with pointed ears. They are a civilization with different timescales, priorities, and categories of meaning.

Possible human interpretations:

- elder cousins
- angels without mercy
- beautiful devils
- soulless immortals
- star people
- keepers of roads humans should not use
- ghosts of an earlier world

None of these should be treated as the full truth.

## Cairnkin

Cairnkin are stonewise mountain and low-cairn peoples whose crafts predate many human kingdoms. Humans need them for roadwork, quarrying, bridge repair, and tomb craft, then blame them when old stones behave strangely.

For Book One, a Cairnkin stonemender may understand roads, burn marks, and boundary stones better than literate human officials.

## Vharakul

Horned border peoples with warrior and herding traditions. Human kingdoms often hire them, fear them, and lie about them. Treat regional prejudice as human politics, not as Aurelith policy.

## Thallor

Fungal and root-minded forest peoples from old wet regions. Use sparingly. They are useful for later stories about memory, ecology, and territory humans cannot own cleanly.

## Dravyr

Dragonlike furnace beings reduced in human knowledge to relics, hearth cults, old weapons, and stories told near mines. Do not make them common village visitors.

## Eirrem

Ghostly remnants tied to failed rites, plague ground, or memory disasters. Keep them rare and local.

## Nacren

Sea peoples whose tide customs and border laws do not fit inland kingdoms. Later stories may use them for trade, pilgrimage, and maritime war.

## Caltrow Kith

Trickster markets and impossible bargains. Keep them folkloric and dangerous. They work best as rumor until a story needs one.

## Open questions

- Which nonhuman peoples are real in Book One's region, and which are only named in stories?
- Which human prejudices are old enough to sound like tradition but recent enough to expose human guilt?
- Which peoples have direct Aurelith contact, and which only inherit rumors?
