# 08 - Technology and infrastructure

## Human material level

Human technology is medieval to high fantasy: carts, mills, forges, ovens, wells, rope bridges, ferries, keeps, abbeys, market roads, toll houses, parchment, wax, iron keys, leather ledgers, banners, bells, spears, bows, swords, plows, looms, and animal medicine.

Humans solve problems with labor, custom, law, violence, craft, superstition, and bargaining.

## Human infrastructure

Important human systems:

- toll roads and bridge rights
- mill monopolies
- grain barns
- war levies
- ransom ledgers
- abbey courts
- market charters
- ferry licenses
- border stones
- beacon fires
- winter stores
- messenger routes

These should drive stories more often than Aurelith works do.

## Aurelith works

Aurelith craft appears rarely and without explanation:

- a road that no horse will step onto
- white stones that never weather
- a tower visible from too many valleys
- a field where arrows fall short
- a relic that noble houses fear but cannot use
- blue-white light with no flame
- impossible geometry that human masons copy badly

Humans do not maintain these works. They route around them, worship near them, exploit rumors about them, or die misreading them.

## What has changed

Do not present Aurelith technology as a daily system for governing human names, schools, villages, songs, or courts. If a later story uses direct Aurelith craft, it should be rare enough to disturb the human world rather than explain it.

## Open questions

- Which ordinary human infrastructure matters most in Book One: toll road, barn, chapel court, or ferry?
- Which Aurelith boundary changes human war routes without appearing to act?
- Which noble house owns a relic it cannot use?
