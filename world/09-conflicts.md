# 09 - Conflicts

## Main conflict philosophy

Most conflict in Neodym should be human before it is Aurelith.

Humans have enough reasons to hurt each other: hunger, pride, debt, succession, faith, revenge, fear, land, inheritance, class, marriage, ransom, border law, and lies that became profitable.

The Aurelith make the world stranger and larger, but they should not be the obvious source of every wound.

## Human conflict types

- border wars over roads, mills, and pastures
- noble houses using old myths to claim land
- peasant villages crushed between rival banners
- abbey courts protecting truth only when paid
- market leagues starving rivals through tolls
- religious factions arguing over elder powers
- old war crimes hidden as saint stories
- bandits wearing lordly colors
- minorities blamed for plague, crop blight, or Aurelith omens
- lords exploiting forbidden boundaries to avoid blame

## Aurelith-shaped conflicts

Aurelith influence works best as pressure at the edge:

- no army will cross a certain field
- a campaign route bends and leaves a village exposed
- an envoy's silence ruins a king's claim
- a relic in a vault becomes a reason for murder
- a roadless territory makes human borders weird
- a priestly argument about elder folk sparks human violence

## Book One conflict

Book One is about a human levy built on a lie. A burned tithe barn, a false accusation, and a boy's name on a war roll drive the plot. Starvel Field shapes the roads and fears around the conflict, but it does not judge the case.

## Open questions

- Which rival houses are fighting near Low Wicker?
- How much does the local lord know about the false fire?
- Which human institution can hear Nera's proof, and why might it fail her?
