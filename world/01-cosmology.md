# 01 — Cosmology and Creation Myth

## The official Aurelith creation myth: The First Stillness

In the beginning, the world was noise. Stone, sea, beast, star, and spirit moved without measure. Nothing remembered itself long enough to become real. Then the **First Stillness** descended from the highest dark: a silver thought wrapped in moonfire. Where it touched the deep earth, it hardened into **Neodym**, the first substance capable of memory.

From Neodym rose the Aurelith, not born but clarified. They were shaped as keepers of continuity. Their duty was to teach the world to remember, to prevent mortal passion from dissolving creation back into noise. Human beings, in this myth, are beloved sparks: warm, unstable, miraculous, and in need of tending.

The Quiet Concord uses this myth to justify rule. If the Aurelith were made as memory's stewards, then human sovereignty becomes a local privilege, not a cosmic right.

## The forbidden human creation myth: The Laughing Flame

Human underground storytellers teach that creation began with a wound in the dark. From the wound came a laughing flame that refused to burn the same way twice. It became the first hearth, the first voice, the first child, the first lie, the first promise, and the first death. The world was not made to be still; it was made to change.

In this version, Neodym is not holy order. It is a splinter of the cold thing that tried to seal the wound. The Aurelith are not firstborn keepers but beautiful custodians who mistook preservation for love. Humans are not sparks to be tended; they are the original fire by which the world remains alive.

Possession of this myth in written form is treated as seditious theology in most human provinces.

## The Thalloran version: The Root That Dreamed Backward

The **Thallor**, fungal memory-people of the drowned forests, believe neither fire nor stillness came first. They say the world is a root dreaming of its own future. Neodym is a mineral knot where the root's dream turned hard. The Aurelith learned to read that hardened dream, but they confused reading with authorship.

The Thallor claim memory grows best when it decays. A perfect archive is a dead forest. A living past must rot, seed, return, contradict itself, and feed new growth.

## The role of Neodym in creation

Different traditions offer conflicting origins:

- **Fallen moon:** Neodym is the marrow of a moon that broke before recorded time.
- **Dead god:** Neodym is the bone of **Meyr**, a god of remembrance edited out of official scripture.
- **Divine shackle:** Neodym was forged to bind a chaos-flame beneath the world.
- **Ancient relic:** Neodym predates the current age and may be the remains of a civilization that transformed itself into mineral memory.

The working truth for story purposes: Neodym is a memory-bearing substance from a broken celestial body that struck Edrath before the rise of recorded peoples. It carries impressions of possible motions, past emotions, and unfinished divine intention. It is not evil, but it makes control easier.

## Myth as weapon

Aurelith archives preserve thousands of human myths, but most are sealed, revised, or classified as childhood variants. Human bards who sing uncatalogued creation songs can be tried for **Origin Distortion**. The Quiet Concord does not erase every myth; it ranks them. Approved stories become folklore. Dangerous stories become pathology.

## Hidden historical truth

All three myths may conceal fragments of one event: a pre-Aurelith age in which humans and other races awakened Neodym through mass ritual, causing a continent-wide disaster called **The Sky Fever**. The Aurelith either emerged from that disaster or used it to claim permanent authority. Their oldest archives contain blank centuries where the record has been deliberately polished smooth.

## Visual notes

- Official myth art: moon-silver geometry, calm faces, ordered stars, Neodym as holy seed.
- Forbidden human myth art: a red-gold flame inside a black wound, hands around a hearth, figures dancing in dangerous joy.
- Thalloran myth art: fungal roots through crystal, memory spores glowing in wet darkness, old faces in bark and lichen.

## Open questions

- Was Meyr a real god, a human name for the fallen moon, or an erased Aurelith ancestor?
- Did humans awaken Neodym first?
- What actually happened during The Sky Fever?
- Which creation myth predicts the end of the current order most accurately?
