# 04 — Politics and Power

## Highest authority: The Quiet Concord

The Quiet Concord rules from **Cyr Veyr**, the crescent archive-capital. It is composed of nine offices:

1. **The First Memory** — custodian of official history.
2. **The Silver Hand** — civil law and provincial administration.
3. **The Mirror Veil** — surveillance, census, and identity.
4. **The Ivory Spear** — military command and border defense.
5. **The Lunary Mother** — Clarification, lineage, and Aurelith continuity.
6. **The Bread Star** — food, medicine, roads, and public welfare.
7. **The Quiet Scale** — courts and punishments.
8. **The Black Orrery** — prophecy engines and crisis prediction.
9. **The Unlit Seat** — officially empty, reserved for catastrophic judgement; secretly active.

The Concord issues decrees called **Mercies**. Every Mercy is written as protection, even when it confiscates children, relocates villages, or silences histories.

## Puppet human rulers

Human provinces are governed by hereditary or elected rulers confirmed by Aurelith seal. These rulers keep local courts, collect taxes, and maintain festivals. Their authority ends wherever prophecy, memory law, or public stability is invoked. The most powerful collaborator families are called **Sealed Houses**.

Privileges for collaborators include warm schools, travel rights, archive access, better medicine, reinforced homes, and the right to petition Aurelith patrons. The cost is complicity.

## Rebel territories

The **Emberwake** does not hold a single nation. It survives as scattered territories:

- hidden mountain hamlets beyond mirror range;
- flooded libraries under old human cities;
- moving camps inside Vharakul borderlands;
- cellar chapels in collaborator capitals;
- sea routes protected by Nacren currents;
- Caltrow markets that appear under impossible bridges.

Rebels hide by becoming socially ordinary: bakers, midwives, gravediggers, musicians, canal workers, shrine keepers, and traveling tinkers.

## Neutral zones and borderlands

Some places resist clear control:

- **Morrowfen:** Thallor root-memory makes prediction decay.
- **Unnumbered Sea:** tides blur mirror paths.
- **Gravewind Marches:** monster roads force Concord reliance on Vharakul clans.
- **Blank Kilns:** memory damage makes governance dangerous.
- **Jester Markets:** Caltrow treaty zones where even Aurelith hesitate.

## Religious institutions

The state religion is the **Doctrine of Continuance**, administered by logic-priests called **Still Cantors**. Approved human saints are allowed if interpreted as servants of peace. Underground faiths worship the Laughing Flame, the Ash Mother, Meyr the Edited God, or unnamed ancestors.

## Military forces

- **Ivory Quiet:** elite Aurelith enforcers with Neodym glaives and mirror armor.
- **Province Watches:** human police under sealed captains.
- **Vharakul Auxiliaries:** border troops and heavy shock companies.
- **Cairnkin Sappers:** tunnel and siege specialists, often coerced.
- **Mercy Choirs:** pacification singers used before and after crackdowns.
- **Emberwake Cells:** decentralized rebels using sabotage, song codes, and memory theft.

## Merchant powers

The **Ledger Houses** move grain, salt, cloth, ore, medicine, and approved memories. Many collaborate openly while funding black markets privately. Their loyalty is to continuity of trade.

## Secret societies

- **The Emberwake:** broad resistance.
- **The Red Loom:** Bloodsong teachers.
- **The Unsealed Hand:** smugglers of identity papers and stolen memories.
- **The Mercy Heresy:** Aurelith dissidents.
- **The Final Clarifiers:** extremists inside the Concord.
- **The Ash Synod:** human zealots who want all Neodym destroyed.

## Law enforcement

Law is enforced through prediction and identity. A citizen's seal records travel, debts, guild membership, family obligations, and risk marks. Courts use mirror testimony and memory extraction. Punishments often look merciful: reassignment, silence vows, memory correction, civic labor, child removal, or relocation to safer districts.

## Education and child selection

Human children are tested at ages seven, twelve, and seventeen in rites called **Measure Days**. Gifts are classified:

- **Green:** safe craft, approved schooling.
- **Silver:** useful talent, state apprenticeship.
- **Black:** unstable memory or Bloodsong tendency, restriction or removal.
- **Red:** prophecy-disruptive, immediate intervention.

This is one of the regime's deepest wounds. It has saved children from dangerous gifts and destroyed families with equal confidence.

## How rebels evade control

- Sing messages in rhythm rather than words.
- Travel during funerals, storms, festivals, and mine shifts.
- Use Caltrow misrule charms to blur seal readings.
- Hide memories in bread yeast, quilt knots, prayer beads, and tooth fillings.
- Swap identities with the consenting dead.
- Work through collaborators who regret too late.

## Hierarchy of peoples

1. Aurelith high offices.
2. Aurelith functional houses.
3. Sealed human collaborators.
4. Useful nonhuman client peoples.
5. Ordinary registered humans.
6. Restricted gifted humans.
7. Unsealed laborers, prisoners, and rebels.
8. Eirrem and other legally ambiguous souls.

## Visual notes

- Politics should be visible in clothing and architecture: seal bracelets, throat rings, mirror checkpoints, bread ledgers, silver court masks, school measuring rods.
- Collaborator spaces should be beautiful but slightly anxious, with human warmth disciplined by Aurelith symmetry.
- Rebel spaces should be hidden in ordinary life rather than grand fortresses.
