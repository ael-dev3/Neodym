# Thin-veil manifesto

## The new spine

Neodym is a mostly human high-fantasy world. Humans build kingdoms, break kingdoms, marry for power, raid fields, lie in court, starve each other, crown fools, bury saints, keep serfs, write songs, and make disasters without needing the Aurelith to manage them.

The Aurelith exist, but they do not sit inside every human village. They are an older civilization with their own timescales and meanings. Most humans never see one. Some human kings may receive a silent envoy once in a reign. A border shepherd may see a blue-white light on a hill twice in a lifetime and never know whether it was a warning, weather, or nothing meant for him.

The human world comes first. The Aurelith are the thin veil at the edge of it.

## What changes

The old direction made the Aurelith feel like active schoolmasters, clerks, wardens, and village administrators. That made human society passive. It also made every story feel like a lesson about elf power.

The new direction makes human life wide again.

- Human rulers govern most human lands.
- Human courts make cruel human judgments.
- Human armies burn human crops.
- Human religions argue over what the Aurelith are.
- Human nobles lie about ancient contact to make themselves look chosen.
- Human peasants may care more about rent, sheep rot, bad roads, and marriage debts than about elves.
- Aurelith works exist as boundaries, ruins, impossible objects, old treaties, and rare diplomatic silences.

## The ratio

Most human stories should use this ratio:

- 95 percent human life, human pressure, human choices, human consequences.
- 5 percent uncanny Aurelith pressure, rumor, taboo, artifact, or distant presence.

That 5 percent should change the shape of the scene without taking the scene over.

## The ant analogy

The Aurelith should feel closer to humans sharing a world with ants than to kings ruling subjects. Humans do not hate ants as political enemies. They do not explain law to ants. They may step around an anthill, study it, preserve it, poison it, ignore it, or build a road through it. If ants mistake the shadow of a boot for a divine judgment, that is the ants' limit.

Use the analogy carefully. Aurelith are not cruel by default. They are frightening because they are not emotionally available to humans.

## Contact levels

Level 0: no contact. Most humans live here.

Level 1: mythic background. Songs, sermons, noble claims, taboo roads, contradictory rumors.

Level 2: environmental presence. Forbidden hills, towers no road reaches, old boundaries, fields armies avoid.

Level 3: artifact contact. A human finds, inherits, steals, misreads, or fears something Aurelith-made.

Level 4: diplomatic contact. Rare envoys to kings, high priests, ancient houses, or treaty courts.

Level 5: direct intervention. So rare it feels like a flood, eclipse, plague, angelic visitation, or natural disaster.

Book One should stay mostly at Levels 0 through 2. Use Level 3 only if the human story needs it. Do not use Level 4 or 5 in Book One.

## The test

For every Aurelith element, ask:

- Can this be a boundary instead of a bureaucracy?
- Can this be rumor instead of fact?
- Can this be a human misreading?
- Can humans cause the actual harm?
- Can the story work if no elf appears on page?
- Can the Aurelith feel stronger by mattering less?

If yes, revise that way.
