# Current problems found in the old direction

## World-level problems

The old canon leaned too hard on direct Aurelith administration. It implied a world where humans were constantly measured, educated, watched, corrected, registered, and ruled through magical bureaucracy. That made the Aurelith too legible and too present.

The result was smaller than intended:

- Human kingdoms felt secondary.
- Human wars felt like exceptions instead of ordinary history.
- Human cruelty was often outsourced to elf systems.
- Village life became a delivery mechanism for silver law.
- The Aurelith began to feel like fantasy schoolmasters and police rather than a distant elder civilization.
- The world read more like a political model than a living fantasy setting.

## Book One problems

The first drafted chapter had useful rural texture, but the story did not start sharply enough.

Problems to fix:

- The opening spent too long on market motion before the dramatic problem arrived.
- Nera's desire on page one was unclear.
- Pell was introduced more as charming trouble than as someone with urgent emotional stakes.
- The plot looked like an elf-law case before it looked like a human story.
- Name-Irons, silver law, and the moot-house bell made the Aurelith machinery too direct too early.
- Market details sometimes decorated the world instead of causing pressure.

## Language problems

Terms that pushed the setting toward over-integration:

- census
- identity seal
- correction
- surveillance
- village administration
- education of humans
- subject species
- prophecy engine as daily politics
- mirror-police implications
- identity systems everywhere

These can still exist as old rumors, elite institutions, or rare Aurelith works if a later saga needs them, but they should not define ordinary human life.

## New fix

The fix is not to make the Aurelith weaker. It is to make them less available.

Humans should be louder, messier, crueler, funnier, and more responsible for their own world. The Aurelith should become older, colder, stranger, and rarer.
