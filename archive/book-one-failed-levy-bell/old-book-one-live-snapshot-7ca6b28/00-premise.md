# 00 - Book One premise

## Working title

Primary working title: **The Field Where No Banners Go**

Other title options:

1. The Field Where No Banners Go
2. The Ash Road Letter
3. The Boy on the Levy Roll
4. No Banner at Starvel Field
5. The Road Around the White Stones

## One-sentence pitch

A low-born village letter-reader must carry proof of a lord's false war levy before an orphaned boy she loves is taken, while every road out of the valley bends around an Aurelith boundary no army dares cross.

## What kind of story this is

Book One is a small human border story. It is about war, debt, witness, cowardice, hunger, and one dangerous letter. The Aurelith are present only as a thin veil: Starvel Field, white stones, bad maps, old taboo, and human fear.

No elf needs to appear on page.

## Back-cover-style premise

Low Wicker is a sheep valley with more mud than glory. Its people know tolls, lambing, rain, debt, chapel fees, and which lord's men are least likely to steal hens on the road.

Nera Brindle earns bread by reading letters, marking bridge tolls, and helping at the Split Loaf ovens. She is useful because she can make words behave. She is small because the words belong to men with seals, swords, and banners.

When levy riders arrive before dawn, Pell Quick's name is on their roll. Pell is thirteen, orphaned, quick with errands, and not old enough for a spear except in the eyes of a lord who needs bodies. Nera tries to hide him in ink.

Then Pell brings her something worse than a summons: a dead carter's letter pulled from the ash of the burned tithe barn. The letter says the fire that justifies the levy was no enemy raid. It was set by Lord Venn's own men.

By nightfall, Low Wicker must give grain, names, and silence. Nera has one chance to carry the proof to a human court before Pell is marched away. The watched road is blocked. The old road skirts Starvel Field, where white stones mark a boundary no army crosses and no story agrees how it began.

Most dangers on the road are human. The field is only the edge of the world reminding everyone how small their banners are.

## Core conflict

A human lord's men use a false fire to justify a war levy and seize control of Low Wicker's road grain. Nera must expose the lie without getting Pell killed, starved, or taken as a fugitive.

## Secret beneath the conflict

The burned tithe barn was set by men who benefit from the levy. They want grain, bodies, and a route advantage in a local border war. The Aurelith boundary is useful to them only because humans fear it and route around it.

## Moral dilemma

If Nera stays quiet, Pell may be taken and the village keeps a narrow peace with the men who can punish it.

If she carries the letter, she may save Pell but bring armed retaliation onto Low Wicker, expose Osa and Sister Auma, and force people to choose truth over winter grain.

## Ending promise

The ending should win one human-sized thing:

- Pell is spared the immediate levy.
- The false fire is exposed enough to blunt one lord's claim.
- Low Wicker keeps part of its grain and loses part of its innocence.
- Nera becomes someone who can carry dangerous truth rather than merely hide it.

The world remains unjust. The war continues elsewhere. Starvel Field remains silent and unexplained.
