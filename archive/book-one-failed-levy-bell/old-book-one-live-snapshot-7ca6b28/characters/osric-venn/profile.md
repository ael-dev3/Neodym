# Osric Venn — Profile

## Role in Story
local debt-holder, bailiff’s man, and human antagonist.

## First Appearance
Chapter 1 - Levy Bell — `book-one/manuscript/chapter-01-levy-bell.md`.

## Later Appearances
1-2, 5, 9-11 planned; drafted in 1.

## Age Impression
middle-aged.

## Social Class / Status
locally risen servant of Lord Venn with money, road access, and borrowed authority.

## Occupation
debt buyer, tithe counter, levy enforcer.

## Physical Description
trim beard, dry mouth, clean boots because poorer men walk ahead of him, watchful eyes.

## Clothing / Material Details
black cloak, bronze boar pin, gloves kept cleaner than the road warrants.

## Public Personality
practical, precise, coldly lawful, never as loud as his power.

## Private Fear
that he is still a miller’s nephew wearing another man’s sign.

## Immediate Desire
make the levy pass cleanly and profit from the false fire.

## Long-Term Desire
turn war into status and cancel the shame of debt.

## Moral Flaw
believes practicality excuses cruelty if paperwork survives.

## Hidden Wound
class ambition sharpened into contempt for those who remind him of where he came from.

## Relationship to Central Conflict
his lie creates the levy and threatens Pell.

## What They Know
who burned the barn, why the timing matters, which records can be bent.

## What They Misunderstand
thinks people will choose bread over truth every time.

## What They Would Never Say Aloud
I need the lie because without it I am small..

## Key Object or Visual Motif
clean boots in mud, bronze boar, dry voice.

## Continuity Notes
Keep this character’s function human, local, and causally tied to the levy-letter conflict. Do not expand them into unrelated lore.
