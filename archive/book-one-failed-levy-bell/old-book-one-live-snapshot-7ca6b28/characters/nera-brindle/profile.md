# Nera Brindle — Profile

## Role in Story
low-born letter-reader, toll scribe, baker’s assistant, and protagonist.

## First Appearance
Chapter 1 - Levy Bell — `book-one/manuscript/chapter-01-levy-bell.md`.

## Later Appearances
1-12 planned; drafted in 1.

## Age Impression
24.

## Social Class / Status
low-born useful woman with literacy but no formal power.

## Occupation
letter-reader, toll scribe, baker’s assistant.

## Physical Description
narrow, quick-eyed, rain-dark hair usually pinned badly, ink stains at the thumb, flour caught in cuffs when she forgets to wash before reading.

## Clothing / Material Details
plain wool dress, patched apron, knife sheath repurposed for a bread knife and scraping tool, cheap leather shoes that take water at the seam.

## Public Personality
useful, dry, clever enough to be dangerous and poor enough to be ignored.

## Private Fear
that speaking plainly will get people she loves punished.

## Immediate Desire
keep Pell from being taken and get the letter heard before the levy leaves.

## Long-Term Desire
become someone who can carry truth without trimming it into safety.

## Moral Flaw
edits truth until protection becomes complicity.

## Hidden Wound
her mother’s debts and Osa’s survival lessons taught her silence as craft.

## Relationship to Central Conflict
she is the only person with enough literacy, courage, and love for Pell to move the proof.

## What They Know
letters, toll rolls, local debts, the difference between record and excuse.

## What They Misunderstand
she thinks quiet can still save Pell after the first bell.

## What They Would Never Say Aloud
I am afraid my cleverness has only taught me how to help cowards..

## Key Object or Visual Motif
scraped ink, bread knife, wet parchment.

## Continuity Notes
Keep this character’s function human, local, and causally tied to the levy-letter conflict. Do not expand them into unrelated lore.
