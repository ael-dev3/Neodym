## Why this direction failed

The prior opening depended on Nera Brindle altering what read like an official levy roll inside the Split Loaf. Even though the scene had urgency, the mechanism invited a skeptical reader to ask why a low-status village reader would have physical access to a lord's actionable levy document, why the document would be in a bakehouse, and why scraping a name could plausibly affect the institution.

That access problem breaks trust before the emotional stakes can work. A story about law, records, and survival cannot begin with a document behaving conveniently.

## Restart rule

The new Book One must be rebuilt from first principles:

1. The protagonist's job must explain what they can access.
2. Every document, room, road, body, or object must have a keeper.
3. Institutions must behave plausibly even when corrupt.
4. The antagonist must act from motive and constraint, not plot need.
5. The Aurelith layer must remain a subtle boundary or rumor, not the machine that solves the story.

## Reusable fragments

These fragments may be reused later only if rebuilt logically:

- Rural pressure: mud, bread, chapel fees, tolls, winter stores, bad roads.
- Low-born literate protagonist type: useful because she reads, vulnerable because she lacks power.
- Hungry child or ward at risk, but only through a plausible institution.
- Hard guardian figure whose love appears as scolding.
- Local official caught between fear and conscience.
- Cairnkin or outsider craft knowledge, if the plot genuinely needs physical expertise.
- Aurelith thin-veil boundary: white stones, no banners, uncertain taboo, human fear.

## Do not reuse without repair

- A protagonist casually editing an official roll.
- Any official document left where it is convenient.
- Aurelith village administration, direct elf court machinery, or miracle resolution.
- A public climax that works only because the setting suddenly invents a rule.
