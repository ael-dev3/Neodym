# Failure analysis: Levy Bell version

## Core plausibility failure

The Chapter 1 hook had force but not institutional credibility. Nera scraping Pell's name from the levy roll implies:

- the roll is accessible before official use;
- Nera has unsupervised physical possession;
- alteration might meaningfully affect conscription;
- the roll's chain of custody is weak enough for a bakehouse intervention;
- the risk is obvious but the access is not explained.

A skeptical owner would rightly challenge all of that.

## Why patching is weaker than restarting

The story could try to retrofit a reason Nera handles local copies, but the initial image has already trained the reader to distrust the mechanism. Retrofitting would make the plot feel defensive. A new premise should choose an institution where the protagonist's access is legitimate from the first sentence.

## Principle for the replacement premise

The protagonist should encounter the plot-critical contradiction while doing their ordinary authorized work. The problem should not be that they steal access; the problem should be what they are morally required to do after access gives them knowledge.

## Useful craft lesson

If the story is about documents, every document needs:

- creator;
- custodian;
- location;
- chain of custody;
- legitimate readers;
- illegitimate readers;
- punishment for tampering;
- practical effect if believed.
