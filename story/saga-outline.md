# Saga outline

## Thin-veil saga principle

The saga is not about humans discovering that every human problem was secretly managed by the Aurelith. It is about a human world becoming more aware that its borders, myths, religions, and oldest political lies formed around a civilization too distant to be reduced to human categories.

Human history stays human. Human guilt stays human. Aurelith power remains real, rare, and difficult to interpret.

## Phase 1: Human ground

Stories begin in villages, roads, market towns, courts, border wars, monasteries, and noble houses. Aurelith presence appears mostly through taboo, ruins, roadless territories, old treaties, and rare artifacts.

Goal: prove the world works as human fantasy before widening the mystery.

## Phase 2: Human myths collide

Different human kingdoms and faiths hold incompatible accounts of the Aurelith. Wars and succession crises begin using those myths as weapons. Some claims are fraud. Some are mistaken. A few may contain dangerous fragments of truth.

Goal: reveal that ignorance is politically useful.

## Phase 3: Artifact and envoy pressure

A small number of Aurelith works or envoys enter high human politics. They do not explain themselves. Their presence makes human categories fail: treaty, miracle, weapon, relic, saint sign, diplomatic insult.

Goal: make contact rare enough to feel world-shaking.

## Phase 4: The elder boundary

Humans learn that some forbidden regions are not arbitrary. They may be ecological preserves, old disaster zones, sacred Aurelith works, containment fields, or none of the names humans use. The truth should remain partial.

Goal: widen wonder without replacing drama with lore.

## Phase 5: Direct intervention, if earned

Only after many human stories have established the world should direct Aurelith intervention occur. It should feel like a natural disaster, angelic visitation, or impossible weather: beautiful, terrifying, and not there to make humans feel important.

## Recurring saga tensions

- humans using Aurelith silence as legitimacy
- human wars bent around forbidden terrain
- relics conferring prestige without understanding
- priests arguing over beings who do not answer theology
- common people paying for noble myths
- rare individuals respected by Aurelith without making humanity equal as a class

## What the saga must avoid

- elves as daily rulers of humanity
- human villages as Aurelith classrooms
- solved Aurelith technology
- universal identity systems
- revelation that all human politics are secretly elf policy
- exposition scenes where elder beings explain the setting
