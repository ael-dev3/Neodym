## Five premise candidates

### 1. The Ninth Candle

- **Protagonist:** Elsbet Vale, low-born chapel copyist.
- **Immediate want:** finish the quarterly fair copy and collect her copy fee before her household debt is called.
- **Small location:** Harrowick Ford, a chapel-ferry village.
- **Central human conflict:** a bailiff is erasing a widow's marriage record to seize a ferry meadow.
- **Institution:** chapel register, candle tallies, manor flood court, wardship law.
- **Why protagonist is involved:** Elsbet lawfully prepares the fair copy whose omission would make the lie official.
- **Why plausible:** her job grants access; the bailiff needs her witness mark; court procedure creates pressure.
- **Antagonist:** Ruan Kest, manor bailiff.
- **Moral dilemma:** sign a safe lie or expose a true record that may ruin her family and chapel.
- **Aurelith thin-veil element:** the Glass Mile, an old white-stone boundary that shapes roads and ferry value but never solves the case.
- **Ending shape:** Wren is protected under temporary chapel ward; transfer is delayed; Elsbet loses safety.
- **Biggest logic risk:** explaining record procedure without slowing the story.
- **Fix:** open on Elsbet making the fair copy under deadline while the bailiff waits.

### 2. The Salt Road Letter

- **Protagonist:** a poor sworn courier.
- **Immediate want:** deliver a paid message and return before market debt closes.
- **Small location:** three villages along a war road.
- **Central human conflict:** a ransom letter would expose a captain holding a prisoner after truce.
- **Institution:** messenger oath, ransom custom, border truce.
- **Why protagonist is involved:** carrying letters is their trade.
- **Why plausible:** access comes from paid delivery and oath.
- **Antagonist:** a local captain profiting from delayed ransom.
- **Moral dilemma:** deliver the letter and free an enemy who killed locals, or suppress it and become part of murder.
- **Aurelith thin-veil element:** a no-banner causeway that armies detour around.
- **Ending shape:** prisoner ransomed, courier marked as oath-breaker or truth-carrier.
- **Biggest logic risk:** travel plot could sprawl.
- **Fix:** limit to one road, one letter, one deadline.

### 3. The Millstone Witness

- **Protagonist:** a miller's apprentice.
- **Immediate want:** keep their apprenticeship after a broken gear ruins grain day.
- **Small location:** a watermill and village court.
- **Central human conflict:** the miller frames a widow's son for sabotage to hide stolen tithe grain.
- **Institution:** mill toll rights, tithe measurement, witness oath.
- **Why protagonist is involved:** apprentice handles grain tallies and sees the false measure.
- **Why plausible:** work access explains evidence access.
- **Antagonist:** miller under debt to soldiers.
- **Moral dilemma:** protect master and job, or expose theft and risk winter bread.
- **Aurelith thin-veil element:** a silent white sluice stone no one repairs.
- **Ending shape:** theft exposed, mill survives under penalty.
- **Biggest logic risk:** grain arithmetic may feel dry.
- **Fix:** make the accused boy and food stakes immediate.

### 4. The Charter at Rainmarket

- **Protagonist:** a market tally clerk.
- **Immediate want:** prove the market may open before spoiled wool ruins sellers.
- **Small location:** a market square under charter dispute.
- **Central human conflict:** a steward alters a market charter to move tolls to a private yard.
- **Institution:** market charter, toll rolls, guild witness.
- **Why protagonist is involved:** clerk copies stall fees and charter readings.
- **Why plausible:** access is public-administrative but limited.
- **Antagonist:** steward redirecting toll income.
- **Moral dilemma:** protect family stall privileges or expose a change that may close market entirely.
- **Aurelith thin-veil element:** old treaty phrase about not raising banners near white stones.
- **Ending shape:** market opens under penalty; clerk loses patronage.
- **Biggest logic risk:** stakes may feel civic rather than intimate.
- **Fix:** tie charter loss to one family eviction or apprentice seizure.

### 5. The Body at Hazel Ford

- **Protagonist:** a washerwoman who prepares the dead.
- **Immediate want:** finish a burial before rain swells the ford.
- **Small location:** ford, chapel, and wash-house.
- **Central human conflict:** a murder is being passed off as drowning to protect a marriage alliance.
- **Institution:** burial witness, dowry contract, local court.
- **Why protagonist is involved:** she lawfully washes the body and sees the wound.
- **Why plausible:** access comes from death work.
- **Antagonist:** a bride's father hiding murder to preserve alliance.
- **Moral dilemma:** expose a murder and destroy a poor girl's marriage security, or bury truth with the body.
- **Aurelith thin-veil element:** the body was found near a forbidden old road that people blame too easily.
- **Ending shape:** murderer exposed, marriage fails, protagonist preserves one dead woman's name.
- **Biggest logic risk:** could become murder mystery genre machinery.
- **Fix:** keep the question moral and local, not puzzle-box.

## Chosen candidate

**The Ninth Candle** is the strongest candidate.

It wins because:

- the protagonist's access is clean;
- the stakes are local but concrete;
- the antagonist has a practical reason to act now;
- the institution can be explained through action;
- the Aurelith layer is truly thin;
- the ending can be narrow and satisfying without solving the world.

## Working title

**The Ninth Candle**

## One-sentence pitch

A low-born chapel copyist preparing a lawful fair copy of birth, marriage, death, and candle records discovers that a bailiff is erasing a widow's marriage to seize a ferry meadow before the flood court, and must decide whether to sign a lie that would keep her family safe or expose a record that powerful humans need buried.

## Story type

Chapel record story with inheritance, wardship, ferry rights, debt leverage, and flood-season court pressure.

## Core answers

1. **Who is the protagonist?** Elsbet Vale, chapel copyist of Harrowick Ford.
2. **What does she want immediately?** Finish the fair copy, collect her fee, and avoid her household debt being called.
3. **What interrupts her?** Ruan Kest demands an early sealed fair copy that omits Maura Dain's marriage.
4. **Why can't she walk away?** Her handwriting and witness mark will make the omission official; refusal threatens her family debt.
5. **What makes the conflict plausible?** Chapel records feed manor court decisions on marriage, legitimacy, wardship, and property.
6. **What happens if she fails?** Wren Dain loses legal identity, cottage, ferry meadow, and freedom of placement.
7. **Why local enough?** One village, one chapel, one ferry meadow, one court hearing, one false omission.
8. **Why does she have access?** She is the appointed copyist.
9. **Why can't a powerful person solve it?** The priest is weak and indebted, the steward needs procedure, the noble is absent, and the child lacks standing.
10. **Why now?** Floodwater makes the ferry meadow valuable and moves the court deadline forward.
11. **Why do characters act as they do?** Debt, fear, record duty, wardship, trade contracts, and survival.
12. **Central moral dilemma?** A safe lie that protects Elsbet's household versus a dangerous truth that protects Wren.
13. **What does Elsbet win?** A delay, Wren's temporary protection, and the preservation of Maura's married name.
14. **What remains unresolved?** Ruan's power, the manor system, Elsbet's future, and the Glass Mile's nature.
15. **How is Aurelith subtle?** The Glass Mile shapes roads and fear; no Aurelith appears, explains, judges, or solves.
