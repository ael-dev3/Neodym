# Prose style guide

## Core voice

Close third person through Nera Brindle. The prose should feel grounded, human, practical, and tense. Open scenes with pressure rather than scenic drift.

## What the prose should privilege

- action that reveals character
- human stakes before lore
- concrete village materials: bread, ash, wool, rain, mud, iron, road, banners, grain, debt, chapel wax
- dialogue with threat under ordinary words
- fear hidden as practicality
- love hidden as scolding
- class pressure and war pressure

## Aurelith restraint

The Aurelith layer should be a thin veil. Use Starvel Field, white stones, lowered banners, bad maps, and human rumor. Do not explain the field. Do not introduce Aurelith officials to solve or frame the plot.

## First-chapter lesson

Do not wander through market texture unless each detail points toward protagonist, conflict, stakes, tone, or theme. The reader should know quickly who Nera is, what she wants, what interrupts her, and why Pell matters.

## Forbidden drift

Avoid:

- active elf bureaucracy
- direct silver-law legal machinery
- magical identity systems
- lore-dump narration
- cute banter that delays danger
- modern political language
- technical explanation
- grand rebellion scale
