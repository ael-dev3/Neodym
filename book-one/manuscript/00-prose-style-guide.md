# Prose Style Guide

## Core voice

Write in close third person through Nera Brindle unless owner feedback explicitly changes the manuscript form. The prose should feel like rural high fantasy observed from ground level: mud, wool, bread, bells, wax, parchment, wet cloaks, bad boots, river stone, smoke, and practical fear.

## Sentence feel

- Prefer concrete nouns and active scene pressure.
- Let humor come from local friction, not modern jokes.
- Keep emotion close to Nera's body and choices.
- Let danger show through what people stop doing: lowered voices, carts moving off the road, children being hushed, hands leaving pockets, songs dying unfinished.
- Avoid grand epic narration except as a distant pressure the villagers cannot reach.

## Lore restraint

The reader may know only what Nera or other local humans can observe, guess, fear, misremember, joke about, or hear as rumor. Do not explain Starvel Hill, the Silver Rider, Aurelith law, or Neodym infrastructure. Surface effects are enough.

## Dialogue rules

Characters should sound practical. They talk about bread, tolls, roads, children, weather, medicine, debts, who will pay, who will be blamed, and what can safely be said. Use subtext: love as scolding, fear as practicality, resentment as jokes, obedience as wisdom.

## Chapter drafting checklist

Each chapter should include:

1. A strong opening image.
2. A local scene goal.
3. Sensory grounding.
4. Nera's internal pressure.
5. At least one practical discrimination detail.
6. At least one small Low Wicker custom or material detail.
7. Dialogue with subtext.
8. No lore explanation beyond scene need.
9. A shift in character pressure.
10. A clean hook or emotional turn at the end.
