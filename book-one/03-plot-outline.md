# 03 — Plot Outline

## Story shape

A contained rural legal mystery with a road adventure inside it.

Escalation path:

1. Personal problem: Pell sings the wrong song at the wrong time.
2. Social problem: the village turns on itself over whether he is worth protecting.
3. Legal problem: silver law requires a record humans cannot produce.
4. Moral problem: saving Pell may endanger a village custom and winter survival.
5. Local crisis: the bridge closure threatens food, medicine, and market debts.
6. Public choice: Nera must speak the truth before the mirror-pillar.
7. Bittersweet resolution: Pell is spared, but the valley pays and the larger order remains untouched.

## Act I — The song and the mark

### Opening image

Market morning at Saint Orra's bridge. Sheep jam the road. Bread carts lean in mud. Pell swings his cracked bell and sings the bridge rhyme for coin. Nera marks toll slips while trying to keep the line moving before rain.

A sealed rider is expected, so humans are already nervous. Everyone knows to keep songs small when silver passes.

### Inciting incident

Pell sings the old last verse, the one Nera's mother told her never to sing near the hill. Starvel Hill answers with a blue-silver hum. The black mirror in the moot-house darkens though it is not in sight. Pell's Name-Iron chills, then leaves a pale ring around his wrist.

### First turn

District clerk Senn Tor reads an edict: the child must be held until Low Wicker proves the song is a licensed custom. If no proof is given by third sundown, Pell will be sent up the Silver Road for correction.

The bridge closes until judgment.

## Act II — The valley starts counting the cost

### Investigation path

Nera searches for lawful proof:

- Toll ledgers in the moot-house: too clean, rewritten after the flood.
- Chapel register: missing pages, burned edges.
- Old bridge stones: moved, mismatched, and older than the current road.
- Market memories: everyone knows the song, but human memory counts for little.
- Bruk's stone knowledge: the river shifted the road, not the forbidden boundary.

### Social pressure

As the closure bites, neighbors shift from sympathy to calculation.

- Osric says flour will spoil and children will go hungry.
- Osa says Pell can survive correction if everyone stops making it worse.
- Joryn warns Nera that hiding Pell will make the whole valley guilty.
- Sister Auma will not testify directly, but gives Nera enough to keep going.

### Midpoint discovery

Nera and Bruk find the old boundary stone half-buried near the ruined watchtower. Its human carving matches a line from the forbidden last verse. The song was not random magic. It was a map humans were allowed to remember only as a children's rhyme.

### Midpoint cost

Bruk is blamed for waking the hill. Pell runs from his hiding place after hearing villagers debate surrendering him. Nera catches him near the lower Silver Road and sees the beacon hum again, not at Pell, but at the misplaced road marker.

The problem is bigger than one child, but still local: the valley road lies across an old line no human authority was allowed to redraw.

## Act III — Judgment at the bridge

### Crisis

Osric admits privately that he helped move the toll marks after the flood. He refuses public confession because it would destroy the mill and his wife may die without district medicine.

Nera can expose him, but doing so may close the bridge all winter. She considers giving Pell to Sister Auma to hide in the marsh, which would save the child but leave the false record intact.

### Public climax

Judgment is held at Saint Orra's bridge because the moot-house cannot fit the crowd. The black mirror-pillar is brought out under cloth. The Silver Rider waits on the hill road, distant and silent.

Nera speaks as toll scribe. Human testimony is discounted. She then sings the Bridge-Bell Rhyme broken into toll counts, child names, and flood dates. Bruk places stones to show the old crossing. Sister Auma reveals the half-burned register without claiming to know why it was hidden.

When Osric repeats the false bridge date, the mirror-pillar turns black. Not because the mirror understands justice, but because the ledger lie is finally spoken in its presence.

### Resolution

Pell is released from immediate correction. The road reopens under penalty. The song is not approved; it is restricted. Children may hum the first lines during crossing, but the last verse must not be sung aloud.

Osric loses face but not the mill. Halven survives by calling the result a correction of toll records, not a challenge to silver law. Osa hides Pell's bell in the oven stones until the watch leaves.

Nera copies the full song into a false flour account and leaves Low Wicker as a road scribe attached to the next market train. She has saved one child and one human memory, not the world.

## What remains unresolved

- What Starvel Hill truly is.
- Why the Aurelith beacon answered the song.
- What correction would have done to Pell.
- Whether the Silver Rider understood more than anyone said.
- Whether the full Bridge-Bell Rhyme can ever be safely sung again.
- Whether Nera's copied song will save someone later or condemn them.

## Why the ending is small but meaningful

The system remains. The road rules remain. Humans still step aside. Starvel Hill still glows.

But Pell stays human, local, named, and present. The village must remember that a child's life was nearly traded for convenience. Nera learns that silence can protect a lie as easily as a person.
