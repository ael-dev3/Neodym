# 04 — Chapter Beats

## Chapter count and target rhythm

Planned chapters: 12.

Estimated final prose: about 22,800 words, averaging 1,700–2,100 words per chapter.

The chapters should read quickly, with practical scene goals, local pressure, and hooks that do not inflate the scale.

---

## Chapter 1 — Market Bell

**Setting:** Saint Orra's bridge and Low Wicker market green.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Establish village texture, Nera's role, Pell's charm, bridge custom, and background fear of silver law.

**What happens:** Nera marks toll slips during sheep market while Pell sings the Bridge-Bell Rhyme for half-coins. A sealed official is expected, making adults tense. Pell adds the old last verse to make children laugh. Starvel Hill answers with a blue-silver hum.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** Everyone steps off the better road before the sealed party even appears. Humans joke about mud being their proper place.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** Bridge tolls are recorded with wax, chalk, and bread tokens because coin is scarce.

**Character change:** Nera moves from irritation at Pell to fear that she taught him something dangerous.

**Hook into next chapter:** Pell's Name-Iron leaves a pale ring around his wrist, and the moot-house bell rings without a hand on the rope.

---

## Chapter 2 — The Mirror in the Moot-House

**Setting:** Low Wicker moot-house.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Turn the strange event into a legal crisis.

**What happens:** Reeve Halven Marr gathers the village. Clerk Senn Tor reads an edict from a sealed frame: unmeasured song near Starvel Hill requires proof of lawful custom. Pell is held under watch. The bridge closes until judgment.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** Human testimony is called remembrance, not record, and cannot outweigh a sealed ledger.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** The black mirror-pillar reflects people half a breath late and darkens when certain statements are made.

**Character change:** Nera realizes being literate does not mean being powerful.

**Hook into next chapter:** Senn gives the village three sundowns to produce proof, then quietly asks Nera whether the last verse has ever been written down.

---

## Chapter 3 — The Split Loaf Argument

**Setting:** The Split Loaf inn and bakehouse.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Show village division and ordinary fear.

**What happens:** Neighbors debate Pell's fate over bread and rainwater ale. Osa Fen argues that correction may be survivable and the road must reopen. Osric Venn counts sacks of flour out loud so no one can avoid the cost.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** A collaborator's cart is allowed through Bramble Gate while human farmers wait with spoiling goods.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** The inn keeps a shelf of approved songs for weddings, funerals, and market openings.

**Character change:** Nera lies by omission, saying she remembers only the safe verses.

**Hook into next chapter:** Sister Auma sends Nera a loaf with a chapel key baked into the heel.

---

## Chapter 4 — Saint Orra's Locked Chest

**Setting:** Saint Orra's chapel.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Begin the investigation through human memory, not grand lore.

**What happens:** Nera searches the chapel register. Pages around the flood year are cut and burned. Sister Auma admits the song was older than the present bridge but refuses to testify directly. Pell, hidden in the choir loft, hears enough to know adults are afraid.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** The chapel's right to bury human dead depends on keeping approved registers clean.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** Children lost to marked roads have river stones placed inside the chapel wall instead of graves.

**Character change:** Nera stops hoping for a clean document and starts looking for a dirty truth.

**Hook into next chapter:** Auma points her toward Bruk, saying stone remembers what paper is paid to forget.

---

## Chapter 5 — Bruk Reads the Road

**Setting:** Mill road, bridge footings, and riverbank.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Add practical investigation and outsider perspective.

**What happens:** Nera hires Bruk to inspect the bridge stones. He shows her flood scars, mismatched foundations, and a moved toll post. Villagers stare and spit because Cairnkin hands near sacred stones make them nervous.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** Humans oppressed by silver law still treat Bruk as a convenient suspect.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** Boundary stones carry human cuts, clerk cuts, and older silver-smooth marks no local can read.

**Character change:** Nera recognizes her own prejudice when Bruk names the stone shifts more honestly than any human clerk.

**Hook into next chapter:** Bruk says the old road did not cross here; it bent toward the ruined watchtower below Starvel Hill.

---

## Chapter 6 — The Ruined Watchtower

**Setting:** Old Ash Copse and the ruined watchtower.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Reveal the song as a practical map while keeping the Aurelith mystery intact.

**What happens:** Nera, Bruk, and Pell slip to the watchtower. They find an old boundary stone half-buried in roots. A line from the last verse matches its carved marks. The beacon on Starvel Hill hums faintly when Pell's bell swings.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** The human map Nera carries shows blank space where the Silver Road should be.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** Local children know forbidden routes as dares and rhymes because maps omit them.

**Character change:** Pell stops finding the adventure funny when he sees Nera afraid of a hill that has no guards.

**Hook into next chapter:** Joryn Hale catches them returning and warns Nera that Osric has accused Bruk of tampering with the boundary.

---

## Chapter 7 — Flour, Fever, and Blame

**Setting:** Osric Venn's mill.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Make the antagonist understandable and increase local cost.

**What happens:** Nera confronts Osric. His wife lies feverish upstairs. The road closure has stopped medicine and grain traffic. Osric admits the bridge toll marks moved after the flood but insists everyone agreed because the old crossing was ruining families.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** District medicine is distributed first to compliant households and last to people under inquiry.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** Mill debts are cut into a wooden tally rail, one notch per sack.

**Character change:** Nera sees that the lie was not born from greed alone, which makes exposing it harder.

**Hook into next chapter:** Osric says if Nera keeps digging, he will swear Pell meant to wake the hill.

---

## Chapter 8 — Oven Stones

**Setting:** The Split Loaf at night.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Force a choice between hiding Pell and fighting the false record.

**What happens:** Pell runs from watch custody. Osa hides him under the warm oven stones despite saying all day that obedience keeps people alive. Nera considers sending him through marsh paths with Bruk, but doing so would leave the bridge lie intact and mark Pell fugitive forever.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** A watch notice says anyone feeding a marked child can lose road rights.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** Bakers use flour marks on doorframes to warn each other when clerks search houses.

**Character change:** Nera chooses public risk over private escape.

**Hook into next chapter:** Sister Auma brings the half-burned register and asks Nera whether she can turn a song into numbers.

---

## Chapter 9 — The Broken Verse

**Setting:** Saint Orra's chapel and empty market green.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Prepare the climax and sharpen symbolism.

**What happens:** Nera reconstructs the Bridge-Bell Rhyme as toll counts, child names, flood dates, and road bends. The last verse is not a spell in the grand sense; it is memory shaped so children would keep it alive. Joryn offers to look away if she flees.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** The approved song shelf contains revised versions with dangerous local names removed.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** Funeral singers sometimes hide names inside rhythm when names cannot be written.

**Character change:** Nera understands that her mother's warning was also a charge: remember, but choose the moment.

**Hook into next chapter:** Dawn brings the Silver Rider to the lower hill road, and every rooster in Low Wicker goes silent.

---

## Chapter 10 — Judgment at Saint Orra's Bridge

**Setting:** Saint Orra's bridge, crowded with villagers, watchmen, sheep pens, and the covered mirror-pillar.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Public confrontation of record, song, and local lie.

**What happens:** Clerk Senn opens judgment. Human memories are dismissed. Nera presents toll books, flood marks, Bruk's stones, and Auma's register. Osric denies moving the record. Nera sings the broken verse as a toll account rather than a forbidden song.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** The clerk warns that a human song can be evidence only if translated into account form.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** The crowd answers old toll phrases under its breath, showing the custom is communal.

**Character change:** Nera stops asking permission to remember.

**Hook into next chapter:** When Osric repeats the false date, the mirror-pillar turns black.

---

## Chapter 11 — The Road Reopens Crooked

**Setting:** Bridge and moot-house after judgment.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Resolve the local legal crisis without making the system kind.

**What happens:** Pell is released from immediate correction. The official result is narrow: toll records corrected, road reopened under watch, last verse restricted. Osric is fined, not ruined. Halven calls it mercy. No one knows whether the Silver Rider approved, ignored, or never cared.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** The edict never says Pell was innocent. It says his removal is deferred.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** A corrected record can still leave a human name stained in red wax.

**Character change:** Nera learns that victory can taste like ash and still be worth having.

**Hook into next chapter:** Pell asks if he is safe now, and no adult answers quickly enough.

---

## Chapter 12 — The Song Under Flour

**Setting:** The Split Loaf, chapel yard, and market cart leaving Low Wicker.

**POV character:** Nera Brindle.

**Chapter purpose:** Bittersweet closure and forward motion.

**What happens:** The village hums only the approved first lines at the bridge. Pell keeps his bell hidden. Osa gives Nera bread for the road. Nera copies the full song into a false flour account and leaves with the market cart as a road scribe, carrying one dangerous human memory beyond the valley.

**Human discrimination detail shown:** Before the cart passes Bramble Gate, Nera steps off the road for a sealed party and notices Pell watching how low she bows.

**Small worldbuilding detail:** False accounts are a human memory practice: recipes, debts, and songs hidden inside one another.

**Character change:** Nera accepts that she cannot free the valley, but she can refuse to let it forget itself completely.

**Final image:** Starvel Hill glows blue-silver behind Low Wicker while the cracked chapel bell gives one bad, human note.
