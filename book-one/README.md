## Working title

**The Ninth Candle**

## One-sentence pitch

A low-born chapel copyist preparing a lawful fair copy of birth, marriage, death, and candle records discovers that a bailiff is erasing a widow's marriage to seize a ferry meadow before the flood court, and must decide whether to sign a lie that would keep her family safe or expose a record that powerful humans need buried.

## Why this premise was chosen

This premise gives the protagonist legitimate access from the first page. Elsbet Vale handles the relevant records because she is the chapel copyist. The antagonist cannot simply steal everything because the institution requires a public fair copy, matching witness customs, and a manor court entry. The conflict is local, human, and mechanically testable.

## Core craft rule

No plot-critical document, place, body, relic, prisoner, or road may be accessible because it is convenient. Access must come from job, oath, kinship, custom, payment, legal role, physical control, or risk.
