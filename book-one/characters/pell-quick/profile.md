# Pell Quick — Profile

## Role in Story
orphaned errand boy whose name on the levy roll makes the conflict personal.

## First Appearance
Chapter 1 - Levy Bell — `book-one/manuscript/chapter-01-levy-bell.md`.

## Later Appearances
1-12 planned; drafted in 1.

## Age Impression
13.

## Social Class / Status
orphaned poor child treated as useful only when others need errands or a body.

## Occupation
errand runner, informal bell-runner, odd-job boy.

## Physical Description
skinny, rain-slick hair, narrow elbows, quick mouth, ash on one cheek in Chapter 1.

## Clothing / Material Details
patched brown coat, too-short sleeves, red wool tied around a cracked handbell.

## Public Personality
nosy, hungry, funny, hard to keep in one place.

## Private Fear
being left behind until he becomes easy to take.

## Immediate Desire
prove he is useful and stay near Nera and Osa.

## Long-Term Desire
belong somewhere without being counted as someone else’s spare body.

## Moral Flaw
runs toward proof before understanding danger.

## Hidden Wound
his mother is gone and his value is always argued by adults.

## Relationship to Central Conflict
his name and witness force Low Wicker to choose between a child and obedience.

## What They Know
what he saw at the tithe barn and where he found the boot letter.

## What They Misunderstand
thinks truth should matter as soon as it is found.

## What They Would Never Say Aloud
If I stop being useful, no one has to keep me..

## Key Object or Visual Motif
cracked handbell tied with red wool.

## Continuity Notes
Keep this character’s function human, local, and causally tied to the levy-letter conflict. Do not expand them into unrelated lore.
