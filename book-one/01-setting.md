# 01 — Setting

## Region

**The Wickerlow Hearthmarch** is a small rural sheep valley in western Edrath, three days by cart from any city that matters and one steep mile below the forbidden silver road to Starvel Hill.

Locals call the valley **Wickerlow**. Officials call it **Hearthmarch Tithe District 17**, which tells the reader everything about how the wider order sees it.

## Scale rule

The entire novella should stay inside this small map:

- Low Wicker village and market green.
- Saint Orra's bridge over the brown River Wicker.
- The Split Loaf inn and bakehouse.
- The chapel of Saint Orra and its cracked bell.
- The moot-house with its black mirror-pillar.
- The mill road and sheep fields.
- Bramble Gate toll bar.
- Old Ash Copse and the ruined watchtower.
- The lower slope of forbidden Starvel Hill.

No city travel. No tour of Aurelith places. No grand map. The world feels large because the characters cannot leave easily.

## Low Wicker village

Low Wicker is built from fieldstone, clay daub, old beams, thatch, slate patches, and smoke-blackened plaster. It has one real street, three muddy lanes, a market green, a sheep pen, a chapel yard, and a bridge that matters more than the village deserves.

Daily textures:

- Wet wool drying near ovens.
- Barley loaves cooling on window boards.
- Sheep bells clacking through fog.
- Wax seals on debt slips.
- Children chasing geese until a clerk passes.
- Old women counting toll marks with knotted string.
- Men stepping off the road with jokes too quiet to be jokes.
- Parchment kept under flour sacks because dry places are rare.

## The road

**The Mill-and-Market Road** enters from the south, crosses Saint Orra's bridge, passes Low Wicker, and climbs north toward Bramble Gate.

A second road, called **the Silver Road**, branches above the village and climbs Starvel Hill. Humans may not walk it without seal, summons, or official escort. Local children dare each other to spit near its first stone but never actually do.

## Inn or common hall

**The Split Loaf** is both inn and bakehouse. Its sign shows one loaf cut unevenly: the larger half painted silver, the smaller half brown. Nobody remembers who first made the joke. Nobody says it in front of collaborators.

The Split Loaf serves as:

- The practical center of gossip.
- Nera's workplace when she is not reading letters.
- The place where travelers hear the bridge song.
- The pressure cooker where villagers debate Pell's fate.
- The safest warm room in the valley, which makes its owner cautious.

## Chapel or shrine

**Saint Orra's Chapel** is older than the current bridge. Saint Orra is remembered as a human road-saint, bell-mother, and finder of children lost in fog. Her chapel has a cracked bell that cannot ring cleanly after rain.

The chapel contains:

- A wall of river stones carved with family names.
- A candle niche for children who never came back from marked roads.
- A locked register chest.
- A painted saint with one hand over her mouth.
- A floor tile that hides a half-burned copy of the old bridge song.

## Seat of local human authority

**The moot-house** is a low timber hall beside the market green. It contains benches, a clerk's desk, a toll scale, a rack for sealed edicts, and a black mirror-pillar set into the north wall.

The mirror-pillar is a visible sign of Aurelith dominance. It is taller than any human in the room. It never speaks. It reflects people half a breath late. Human testimony given before it is treated as lesser if the mirror darkens.

## Forbidden place

**Starvel Hill** rises beyond the fields. Sheep will not graze near its lower stones. At night, a blue-silver light sometimes glows above the bracken, high enough to be seen from the chapel roof.

Humans know almost nothing true about Starvel Hill. Rumors include:

- It marks an old border no human map shows.
- It drinks songs.
- It keeps a list of children who hear wrong.
- It was a shrine before the Aurelith came.
- It is not a hill at all, but a buried door.

No explanation should settle these rumors.

## Natural boundary

The **River Wicker** is brown, quick, and full of reeds. It floods in spring, moves stones, eats banks, and slowly makes liars of maps.

This matters to the plot: the road, bridge, toll marks, and forbidden boundary no longer align cleanly.

## Local custom that conflicts with silver law

The **Bridge-Bell Rhyme** must be sung when children, sheep, or funeral carts cross Saint Orra's bridge.

Locals believe the rhyme asks the saint to count everyone across and leave no soul in the fog. The older truth is that the rhyme preserved a human memory of where the dangerous hill-boundary began.

Silver law does not care why humans sing. It hears unmeasured song near Starvel Hill and treats it as a problem.

## Visible Aurelith dominance

- The black mirror-pillar in the moot-house.
- The blue-silver beacon on Starvel Hill.
- The Silver Road humans step away from.
- Name-Irons checked at Bramble Gate.
- Edicts read by human clerks from sealed silver frames.
- A single distant silver rider who passes through without needing to threaten anyone.

The story should never enter the place from which these things are governed.
