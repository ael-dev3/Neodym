# 05 — Human Perspective Rules

## Absolute POV rule

Book One is human POV only.

Allowed formats:

- Close third person through Nera Brindle.
- Occasional written fragments by Nera if later drafts use account-book notes or copied songs.
- Human rumor, testimony, letters, songs, edicts read aloud by human clerks.

Not allowed:

- Aurelith POV.
- Private Aurelith conversations.
- Scenes inside Aurelith councils, archives, courts, or cities.
- Authorial explanation of what the Aurelith really intend.
- Full explanation of Neodym, Starvel Hill, the beacon, or silver law.

## Knowledge boundary

The reader can know only what grounded human characters can observe, fear, misremember, joke about, or infer.

Humans may see:

- A silver figure passing on the road.
- The mirror-pillar darkening.
- A sealed edict read by a human clerk.
- Blue-silver light over Starvel Hill.
- A Name-Iron cooling against skin.
- A road they cannot walk.
- A blank place on a human map.

Humans may not receive:

- Technical explanation.
- Elven political context.
- The true nature of Starvel Hill.
- The full structure of the Quiet Concord or Mirror Choir.
- Aurelith emotional interiority.
- Reliable answers about what correction means.

## Language rules

Use grounded human nouns first:

- bread
- mud
- wool
- iron
- smoke
- bells
- fields
- old songs
- bad roads
- local grudges
- court days
- harvest debts
- family shame
- candles
- horses
- rain
- worn boots
- sealed papers
- chapel walls
- forbidden hills

Use Aurelith-adjacent language as human naming, not explanation:

- silver law
- moon-metal
- star-glass
- silent beacons
- mirror shrines
- oath geometry
- cold miracles
- hill-light
- sealed roads
- unreadable mercy

Avoid in in-world prose:

- modern political jargon
- technical explanation
- direct metaphors from contemporary computing
- advanced human infrastructure
- detailed elven taxonomy
- lore encyclopedia tone
- continent-scale strategy talk

## Discrimination through action

Show human second-class status through practical behavior:

- Nera steps off the road before a sealed official passes.
- Human testimony is treated as memory, not record.
- Pell is not told what his mark means.
- Osa must keep approved songs on a shelf.
- The chapel can lose burial rights for preserving the wrong verse.
- Bruk is searched first because outsiders are easier to blame.
- Human maps omit the roads that matter.
- A clerk may correct a human name in red wax.
- Medicine follows compliance.

Avoid speeches that explain oppression abstractly. Let characters be tired, funny, resigned, petty, and afraid inside the rules.

## Aurelith distance rules

The Aurelith should feel powerful because they do not need to appear often.

Allowed appearances:

- The Silver Rider seen from a distance.
- The beacon on Starvel Hill.
- The mirror-pillar in human court.
- A sealed edict and silver frame.
- Social reactions to Aurelith authority.

Not allowed:

- Detailed Aurelith society.
- Named elven factions beyond established background terms where absolutely necessary.
- Aurelith explanations of their own motives.
- Direct instruction scenes where an Aurelith teaches the protagonist the truth.
- Aurelith ally guiding the plot.

## Small-scale guardrails

The story must remain about Low Wicker, Pell, the bridge song, and Nera's choice.

Do not add:

- A rebellion arc.
- A world-saving prophecy.
- Large battles.
- New major regions.
- New point-of-view characters.
- A journey to an Aurelith city.
- Full Neodym explanation.
- Advanced human craft beyond medieval or high-fantasy village life.

## Daily revision questions

Every improvement pass should ask:

1. Can this become more human without getting longer?
2. Can the discrimination appear through a choice, fee, gesture, delay, or humiliation?
3. Can the Aurelith feel colder by being less explained?
4. Can a generic detail become a local Low Wicker custom?
5. Can a weak scene be compressed into a stronger scene?
6. Does every addition serve character, plot, theme, or atmosphere?
