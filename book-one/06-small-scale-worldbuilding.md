# 06 — Small-Scale Worldbuilding

## What belongs on-page

Book One should show the Neodym world through village-level systems.

On-page worldbuilding should include:

- Bridge tolls.
- Bread ledgers.
- Name-Irons.
- Approved song shelves.
- Road permits.
- Chapel burial rights.
- Human testimony being devalued.
- Market debt and winter food pressure.
- A local custom under threat.
- A distant silver sign no one can explain.

## What stays off-page

Keep these mostly off-page or only implied:

- Aurelith cities.
- The internal hierarchy of the Quiet Concord.
- The mechanics of the Mirror Choir.
- The origin and full nature of Neodym.
- Continental rebellion networks.
- Grand historical wars.
- Deep metaphysics.
- Other major races unless they are already in the small cast.

## Low Wicker customs

### Bridge-Bell Rhyme

Sung when sheep, children, funeral carts, or wedding parties cross Saint Orra's bridge.

Public meaning: Saint Orra counts everyone safely across.

Hidden practical meaning: the rhyme preserves memory of an older boundary near Starvel Hill.

Plot consequence: Pell's use of the last verse wakes the hill beacon and exposes the mismatch between the moved road and the old forbidden line.

### Split Loaf hospitality

At the Split Loaf, travelers receive the smaller half of the first loaf and locals receive the larger half. During official visits, this reverses. Everyone notices. No one says why.

Plot consequence: Osa can hide messages in who gets which half.

### Flour marks

Bakers mark doorframes with tiny flour strokes:

- One stroke: clerk in the lane.
- Two strokes: road watch searching.
- Crossed stroke: marked child or fugitive inside.

Plot consequence: Osa's one costly mercy is shown through a flour mark she wipes away before the watch sees it.

### River-stone names

Families place carved river stones in Saint Orra's wall for children who vanished under road law or correction. The stones are not official graves.

Plot consequence: Pell sees how many names adults avoid saying.

### Toll counting by song

Older toll keepers once used sung counts because many villagers could not read. The practice is now suspicious because unapproved human song near silver markers can be punished.

Plot consequence: Nera turns the forbidden verse into an account to make it admissible.

## Human legal restrictions

- Humans cannot walk the Silver Road without seal or summons.
- Human maps omit sealed roads and certain hill markers.
- Unapproved public songs can be fined, silenced, or marked.
- Human testimony counts less than sealed record.
- Chapel registers must use approved names and approved causes of death.
- Bread ledgers can be inspected for signs of hidden gatherings.
- Marked children may be held for correction without families being told the full reason.

## Local economy

Low Wicker depends on:

- Sheep fair tolls.
- Barley flour from Osric's mill.
- Road traffic to Bramble Gate.
- District medicine delivered through compliant households.
- Chapel licenses for burial, marriage, and naming.
- Weather and flood cycles that outlast every record.

This keeps the conflict morally grounded. Protecting Pell has a real cost.

## Small objects with emotional weight

- Pell's cracked handbell tied with red wool.
- Nera's toll knife for cutting wax.
- Sister Auma's half-burned register page.
- Bruk's stone chalk.
- Osa's oven peel with old scorch marks.
- Osric's tally rail of flour debts.
- The false flour account where Nera hides the full song.

## Repeated phrase

Potential repeated phrase: **"Count them over, count them home."**

First meaning: a bridge rhyme for children and sheep.

Middle meaning: a legal accounting trick.

Ending meaning: Nera's private vow to carry human names where records try to thin them.

## Humor and warmth

The story should not be pure misery. Use grounded village humor:

- Pell charging half-price for unlucky blessings.
- Sheep blocking official carts with better courage than humans.
- Osa insulting bread quality as a love language.
- Bruk pretending not to understand human superstition when it saves him from chores.
- Nera correcting love letters for grammar while refusing to improve the lies.

Humor should make the pressure hurt more because the people feel alive.
