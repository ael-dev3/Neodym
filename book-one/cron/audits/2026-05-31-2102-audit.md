## Full Story Sense Check
The current story is about Elsbet Vale, a low-born chapel copyist in Harrowick Ford, discovering that Ruan Kest is trying to make a false fair copy erase Maura Dain's marriage so Wren Dain can be treated as an unsecured child and Nine-Candle Meadow can be controlled before the flood court. Elsbet is the protagonist. The central human conflict is not magic or rebellion but whether a poor copyist will make an omission lawful when the truth endangers her brother, the chapel, Matra's trade, and Wren's immediate safety. The emotional spine is Elsbet learning that careful neutrality can become another instrument of erasure.

Elsbet wants to finish the fair copy safely and keep Tomas's mill bond from being used against him. By the end she has chosen a narrower but truer duty: she writes a lawful margin cause that prevents Maura Dain's name from being cleanly erased, even though it does not fully save Wren, Tomas, the chapel, or the meadow. The essential chapters are 1 through 3 for the record and Wren, 4 for the candle slat, 5 for the ferry value, 6 for Orlan's debt and custody, 8 through 10 for the moral temptation and public hearing, and 12 for the margin-note payoff. Chapter 7 is essential only because it keeps the Glass Mile thin and makes route pressure public. Chapter 11 is essential as costly aftermath. Chapter 5 and Chapter 8 remain the weakest from a compression standpoint: both are coherent, but they still contain repeated procedural and explanatory phrasing after the reader already understands proof limits and Ruan's leverage.

The ending pays off the beginning: the fair copy that began as a clean instrument of omission becomes a marginal record of disputed truth. The middle makes the ending more inevitable by accumulating limited proof rather than introducing a convenient solution. No chapter currently makes the whole story less coherent, so nothing should be archived this hour.
