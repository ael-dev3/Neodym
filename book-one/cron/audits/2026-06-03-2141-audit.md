## Full Story Sense Check
The story is currently about Elsbet Vale, a low-born chapel copyist, discovering that Ruan Kest is trying to make Maura Dain's marriage vanish from the fair copy so Wren Dain can be treated as legally weaker and Nine-Candle Meadow can be controlled before flood court. The protagonist is Elsbet. The central human conflict is whether a poor copyist will let her lawful hand make a profitable omission clean when refusing endangers her brother, the chapel, Matra, and Wren. The emotional spine is Elsbet learning that careful writing is not neutral when fear chooses what gets omitted.

Elsbet wants first to finish the copy and protect Tomas; by the end she chooses a limited public record that preserves Maura Dain's name without pretending the system is healed. The beginning sets up the fair copy, Ruan's pressure, Tomas's bond, and the ninth candle. The middle gathers proof through costly human channels: Wren's cord, Matra's slat, the ferry meadow, Orlan's custody, the Glass Mile bend, and Ruan's private compromise. The ending pays off the beginning by putting Maura Dain into the margin where absence was ordered, while Tomas's bond and Ruan's power remain unresolved.

Essential chapters: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11, and 12. Chapter 7 is essential for route economics and thin-veil restraint but still carries the highest risk of feeling like an excursion. Chapter 12 is emotionally right but slightly repetitive in the final third: several sentences restate that the margin note is not enough, that tomorrow remains dangerous, and that the Glass Mile will not help. The ending works, but it can become cleaner if that repetition is trimmed rather than expanded.
