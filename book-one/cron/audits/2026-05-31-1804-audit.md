## Full Story Sense Check
The story is currently about Elsbet Vale, a poor chapel copyist, refusing to make a false fair copy that would erase Maura Dain's marriage and let Ruan Kest take Wren's ferry meadow through wardship. The protagonist is Elsbet. The central human conflict is record custody and debt pressure versus a child's lawful name and small inheritance. The emotional spine is Elsbet learning that neutrality and careful paperwork can become complicity, then choosing to make the lie public even though her brother Tomas is exposed to retaliation. Elsbet wants to keep her hand clean and her household safe; by the end she accepts that no clean safety exists, only honest marks that force powerful people to dirty the roll in public.

The beginning works: Chapter 1 quickly establishes Elsbet's access, Ruan's pressure, Orlan's fear, the marriage line, and the ninth candle. The middle generally makes the ending more inevitable by building proof through Wren's cord, Matra's slat, the ferry meadow, Orlan's key, and public court pressure. Chapters 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, and 12 feel essential. Chapters 5 and 7 are useful but slightly expansive around route/ferry evidence. Chapter 11 is essential in function but currently repeats court-order mechanics and could be sharper after Chapter 10's strong procedural climax. The ending pays off the first blank margin: Maura Dain is not saved completely, but the false omission is stopped from becoming clean law.

The rough register stays under chapel custody; the fair copy is due for court; the manor chest remains inaccessible; Matra's original slat remains with Matra; copied slat and tally board are put under chapel key; Wren keeps the cord because it is hers. ## Small-Scale Fantasy Check
