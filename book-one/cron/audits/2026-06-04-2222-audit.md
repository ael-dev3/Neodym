## Full Story Sense Check
The story is currently about Elsbet Vale, a poor chapel copyist, refusing to let Ruan Kest turn Maura Dain's marriage and Wren's inheritance into a clean omission. The protagonist is Elsbet. The central human conflict is not magic or rebellion but record power: who gets to turn grief, debt, custom, and names into lawful text. The emotional spine is Elsbet discovering that truthful writing costs real people, especially Tomas, but that fear cannot be allowed to choose every word.

Elsbet wants to make the quarterly fair copy without becoming a witness to theft, then wants to keep Wren and Maura from being erased before a court that values clean forms over poor lives. By the end, she has not won the meadow outright, but she has forced the omission into public view, made the court delay rather than transfer, and written a margin cause that prevents the fair copy from pretending Maura was never Dain. She changes from someone whose safety lies in copyist invisibility to someone whose name is now attached to contested truth.

Essential chapters: Chapters 1-3 establish the false copy, Wren's threat, Elsbet's access, and the personal debt cost; Chapters 4-6 gather candle, ferry, custody, and priestly-debt proof; Chapters 8-10 carry the private pressure and public court turn; Chapter 12 gives the right ending image in the margin. Chapter 7 is useful but slightly at risk of feeling like a Glass Mile set piece unless every road detail keeps feeding the witness/ferry logic. Chapter 11 is necessary aftermath but currently has the most room to sharpen Elsbet's emotional cost and bridge the court delay to the final margin writing. The ending pays off the beginning: the clean blank in Chapter 1 becomes the contested margin in Chapter 12. The middle generally makes the ending inevitable by building a proof chain and social witness chain. No chapter makes the story incoherent, but the completed draft is just under compression threshold and needs tightening rather than expansion.

## Grounded Logic Check
The plot mechanism makes institutional sense. The rough register is under chapel custody; the fair copy needs Elsbet's hand and Father Orlan's seal; the manor chest remains inaccessible; Matra's slats are private trade records; Wren's cord and tally are household objects that support but cannot alone prove title; Harl can delay and order production but not magically deliver moral justice. Elsbet has a believable reason to be involved because her appointed copyist role makes the false omission her legal and moral act if she writes it. More powerful people cannot simply solve it because they benefit from delay, fear scandal, or care more about clean procedure than the poor child's name. Characters are acting from motive: Ruan wants road income and clean accounts, Orlan fears losing the chapel seal, Matra fears losing her contract, Wren wants her mother named and her home preserved, Harl wants an appeal-proof roll, and Elsbet is caught between Wren and Tomas.

Remaining logic pressure: the ending must keep reminding the reader that delay is not triumph. Ruan's retaliation against Tomas should feel like a consequence of Elsbet's public choice, not a loose sequel hook.

## Small-Scale Fantasy Check
This feels like a compact human novella: a chapel, ford, ferry, meadow, mill, manor court, road bend, candle shed, and wet village under flood pressure. Stakes are meaningful but local: a child's wardship, a ferry meadow, a brother's bond, a chapel's seal, a bailiff's accounts. There are not too many factions on page. The story stays near local road/village/court/chapel dispute rather than epic saga.

## Aurelith / Magic Rarity Check
