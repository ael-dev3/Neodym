## Full Story Sense Check
The story is currently about Elsbet Vale, a poor chapel copyist in Harrowick Ford, refusing to let Ruan Kest turn Maura Dain's marriage and Wren Dain's ferry claim into a clean omission before flood court. Elsbet is the protagonist. The central human conflict is a local record-and-wardship fight over whether a child can be stripped of name, household, and ferry bank through procedure, debt pressure, and frightened silence. The emotional spine is Elsbet learning that a careful hand can either protect the vulnerable or make cowardice lawful, while every choice risks her brother Tomas, Father Orlan's chapel seal, Matra's livelihood, and Wren's remaining claim to her mother's name.

Elsbet wants to make a fair copy that does not lie. By the end she has not won the meadow or defeated Ruan, but she has made the omission public, kept Maura Dain's name in the margin, and accepted that the cost lands on living people she loves. The essential chapters are 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 11, and 12 because they establish access, living stakes, proof custody, meadow value, Orlan's debt, Ruan's private compromise, the public candle count, the cost after court, and the margin-note ending. Chapters 2, 7, and 9 are also functional but carry more procedural risk: Chapter 2 explains candle custom, Chapter 7 handles route/Glass Mile pressure, and Chapter 9 sets the hearing table. The ending pays off the beginning by turning the first blank omission into a lawful margin cause. The middle makes that ending more inevitable by gathering limited, custody-bound proofs rather than convenient rescue. No active chapter currently makes the story less coherent, but Chapter 7 is still the softest place where travel, taboo-road atmosphere, and witness setup can feel slower than the court pressure around it.

## Grounded Logic Check
The plot mechanism makes institutional sense: Elsbet can access the rough register because she is the appointed chapel copyist; the fair copy is needed for court; Ruan cannot seize the rough book without scandal, so he pressures the copy, seal, witnesses, and debts. Important objects have custody rules: rough register and slat copy under chapel key, Matra's original slat in her shed, Wren's cord as household property, ferry tally from Wren's chest, manor wording from Ruan's clerk, and court roll under Harl Brey. Elsbet is involved because her hand makes the fair copy lawful and therefore potentially false. More powerful people cannot simply solve it: Harl only wants a clean roll, Lady Merrow is absent, Orlan is indebted and half-blind, Matra risks her contract, and Wren lacks standing. If Elsbet is caught or discredited, Tomas's bond, the chapel seal, Matra's contract, and Wren's custody all suffer. Events happen now because floodwater, court timing, ferry value, quarterly copying, and road income converge. Characters act from human motives: Ruan from income/account fear and ambition, Elsbet from duty and guilt, Wren from survival, Orlan from institutional fear, Matra from livelihood and conscience. No current major “why would this happen?” failure blocks the story.

## Small-Scale Fantasy Check
This reads as a small human novella: chapel, ferry, mill, candle shed, flooded meadow, manor court, poor records, debt, wardship, and village witnesses. Stakes are meaningful but not world-ending. There are not too many factions on page; the active forces are the chapel, manor court, ferry household, candle trade, mill debt, and frightened villagers. It feels closer to a local road/village/court dispute than an epic saga. Chapter 7 is the one place where thin-veil scenery could pull attention away from local pressure if left too spacious, so the revision should keep the Glass Mile as route economics and social fear, not wonder.

## Aurelith / Magic Rarity Check
The Aurelith are absent on-page. The Glass Mile is never explained and never intervenes; it changes behavior because people fear the old white stones and route customs, not because magic acts. The story still works if the Glass Mile hints are removed, because the central drama is Maura's name, Wren's wardship, Ruan's ferry timing, and Elsbet's fair copy. No magic is available or convenient. The best next edit should preserve the taboo road as a pressure on maps, animals, cloth, and local speech while cutting any extra atmospheric drag.
