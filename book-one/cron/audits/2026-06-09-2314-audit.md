## Full Story Sense Check
The story is currently about Elsbet Vale, a poor chapel copyist, being asked to make a clean fair copy that would erase Maura Dain's marriage and push Maura's daughter Wren toward wardship while Ruan Kest secures the valuable ferry meadow during flood season. Elsbet is the protagonist; the central human conflict is whether a low-status record keeper will risk her brother's bond, the chapel's seal, and her own fragile usefulness to keep a dead woman's name from being softened into legal absence. The emotional spine is Elsbet learning that not lying privately is not enough: she must make a room witness the knife in the wording, even when the cost falls on people she loves.

Elsbet wants at first to survive the day's copy work without endangering Tomas; by the end, she wants the truth to be entered in a way that cannot pretend to be cleaner than it is. What changes from beginning to end is not the whole legal order but the position of the lie: Ruan's omission fails to become an uncontested fair copy, Wren becomes protected only temporarily, and Elsbet loses safe invisibility while gaining moral agency.

Essential chapters: 1 establishes access, threat, and the missing husband; 2 adds Tomas's bond and the ninth candle; 3 gives Wren agency and the cord; 4 brings Matra's witness and economic pressure; 5 makes the meadow/ferry materially important; 6 reveals Orlan's key, chapel debt, and custody rules; 7 keeps the Glass Mile as route pressure while adding public witnesses; 8 forces Elsbet's private choice; 9 formalizes the court hearing; 10 earns the temporary court order; 11 shows the costs after partial success; 12 lands the margin cause and ending.

Weakest material: Chapter 10 is effective but slightly overlong and occasionally repeats ideas the surrounding chapters have already made clear: the room's hunger, the partial nature of the victory, and the “not enough / not victory” emotional accounting. Chapter 7 uses the Glass Mile correctly but should remain watched in future passes so its strangeness never crowds out the human legal struggle. Chapter 11's post-court costs are necessary, though future revision could trim some explanatory reflection if the draft needs more compression.

The ending pays off the beginning: the fair copy that begins as a clean surface for omission ends with a narrow margin cause that refuses erasure. The middle makes the ending more inevitable by gathering lawful-but-fragile proof through Wren, Matra, Orlan, the meadow, and public witnesses. No active chapter currently makes the story make less sense; the needed work is sharpening rather than throwing out.

## Grounded Logic Check
The plot mechanism largely makes institutional sense. The rough register stays under chapel custody; the fair copy is Elsbet's appointed task; the manor wording is plausible because Ruan needs agreement rather than open seizure; Matra's trade slat is useful but not overclaimed; Wren's cord supports but does not decide anything; the ferry meadow's value spikes because floodwater and the broken bridge make old toll rights immediately practical. Elsbet has believable access through her copyist role and believable pressure through Tomas's bond. More powerful people cannot simply solve the problem because Lady Merrow is absent, Harl Brey is procedural and self-protective rather than a moral rescuer, and Ruan operates through forms, deadlines, and debt. Characters act from motive: Ruan wants controllable ferry income and clean accounts, Orlan fears losing the chapel seal, Matra fears losing her contract, Wren wants her mother's name and home, Elsbet wants both truth and Tomas safe. The biggest “why?” risk is making the court's partial delay feel like justice; the manuscript mostly avoids that by showing costs immediately.

## Small-Scale Fantasy Check
Yes, this remains a small human novella. Its core materials are village chapel, ferry, court, inn/road equivalents, debt, witness, candle customs, field/meadow, flood, family shame, and a local bailiff's pressure. The stakes are meaningful but not world-ending: a child's name, wardship, ferry income, a brother's labor bond, a chapel's record rights. There are not too many factions on page. The only wider-world pressure is war-road/flood-road economics and the taboo Glass Mile, both kept in the background. It feels closer to a local road/court/ferry dispute than an epic saga.
