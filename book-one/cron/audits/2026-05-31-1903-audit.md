## Full Story Sense Check
The story is about Elsbet Vale, a poor chapel copyist whose lawful work on the quarterly fair copy makes her the person Ruan Kest needs in order to erase Maura Dain's marriage and seize Nine-Candle Meadow's ferry value. The protagonist is Elsbet. The central human conflict is whether a clean-looking omission will turn local debt, wardship, court procedure, and ferry necessity into legal theft. The emotional spine is Elsbet learning that accurate handwriting is not neutral when frightened people need her to sign away someone poorer and more exposed. Elsbet wants to finish the copy, protect Tomas, keep the chapel safe, and avoid becoming the hand that erases Wren's mother. By the end she has not won justice, but she has made the lie visible, preserved Maura Dain's name in the margin, and accepted that her own safety has been entered into the conflict.

Essential chapters: 1 establishes legitimate access and Ruan's demand; 2 establishes the ninth candle and Tomas's bond; 3 makes Wren active; 4 adds Matra's limited slat proof; 5 makes the meadow's ferry value concrete; 6 locks custody and exposes Orlan's debt; 7 ties the Glass Mile to route pressure; 8 tests Elsbet with Ruan's tempting compromise; 9 converts private objection into formal cause; 10 provides the public candle-count delay; 11 makes the aftermath costly; 12 lands the lawful margin-note ending. The ending pays off the beginning's fair-copy omission, and the middle steadily makes the ending more inevitable through custody, debt, witness limits, and flood urgency. No active chapter currently makes the whole story less coherent, but the completed draft is over the compression threshold and Chapter 2 repeats some candle-custom and fear-language beats after the reader has understood the mechanism.

## Grounded Logic Check
The plot mechanism still makes institutional sense. The rough register belongs to the chapel; Elsbet has access because she is the appointed copyist; Ruan needs a matching fair copy because the manor chest alone is vulnerable; Matra controls private slats; Wren controls household objects; Harl delays only because dirty procedure can become expensive. The protagonist has a believable reason to be involved because her hand and witness mark are required. More powerful people cannot solve it cleanly: Orlan is indebted and afraid for the seal, Harl cares about clean rolls rather than justice, Lady Merrow is absent, and Wren lacks legal standing. Events are causally connected by flood timing, court deadlines, wardship, ferry income, and debt. The main live weakness is pacing, not mechanism.
