## Full Story Sense Check
The current novella is about Elsbet Vale, a poor chapel copyist in Harrowick Ford, refusing to let Ruan Kest turn a contradictory chapel record into a clean legal erasure of Maura Dain's marriage and Wren Dain's ferry/meadow claim. The protagonist is Elsbet. The central human conflict is whether a local bailiff can use debt, custody, court form, and flood urgency to make theft look administrative before poor witnesses can be heard. The emotional spine is Elsbet learning that protecting her own brother's bond by silence would spend Wren's name, then choosing a public, partial truth that costs everyone but keeps Maura from being erased. Elsbet wants first to survive the copying deadline and protect Tomas; by the end she wants the record to carry enough truth that fear cannot write every word for her. What changes is not the whole legal order, but the public cleanliness of Ruan's lie: the fair copy receives a margin cause, Wren remains in temporary chapel keeping, the ferry stays under old tolls, and Maura Dain is written where absence had been ordered.

Essential chapters: 1 opens the pressure, access rules, Ruan's omission, and Elsbet's household debt; 2 finds the ninth candle and brings Tomas's bond on-page; 3 gives Wren agency and the marriage cord; 4 brings Matra and copy-slat custody; 5 makes the meadow/ferry value concrete; 6 reveals Orlan's debt and locks the evidence under key; 7 uses the Glass Mile as route pressure and public-witness pressure without making it plot magic; 8 forces Elsbet to refuse Ruan's private compromise; 9 frames the court hearing; 10 wins the partial delay; 11 shows the cost to Tomas and the temporary ward status; 12 pays off the margin-note ending.

Weak/redundant areas: the draft is coherent, but Chapters 5–8 and 10–12 remain long for a compression-threshold manuscript. Chapter 6 is functional and emotionally important, but it repeats the custody/fear/key idea in several adjacent beats and can be tightened without changing plot. Chapter 10 is longest and should remain a later compression target because it carries the public hearing climax. No chapter currently needs to be thrown out. The ending pays off the beginning: fair-copy cleanliness becomes lawful margin truth. The middle makes the ending more inevitable by stacking proof, public witnesses, and Ruan's procedural pressure. No chapter makes the story less sensible, but excess repetition risks slowing the courtward momentum.
