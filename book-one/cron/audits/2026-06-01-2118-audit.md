## Full Story Sense Check
The story is currently about Elsbet Vale, a low-born chapel copyist, refusing to let her fair-copy hand make Maura Dain's marriage disappear so Ruan Kest can take Wren Dain's ferry meadow through wardship and flood-court procedure. Elsbet is the protagonist. The central human conflict is a contest over records, debt, custody, and court procedure: whether a dead woman's married name and a living child's claim can survive when local authority wants a clean omission.

The emotional spine is Elsbet learning that neutrality is a choice when her handwriting can spend another person's life. She wants to finish her lawful work, keep her brother safe, and avoid turning a lie into clean paper; by the end she accepts public risk and writes a limited margin cause that preserves Maura's name without pretending the system is fixed. The story moves from private chapel copying to public court challenge to narrow lawful margin note.

Essential chapters: 1 establishes lawful access and the false fair copy; 2 establishes the ninth candle and Tomas's cost; 3 makes Wren active; 4 provides Matra's limited proof and risk; 5 makes the meadow practical road power; 6 grounds Orlan's fear and custody; 7 gives the Glass Mile thin-veil route pressure; 8 creates the strongest moral temptation; 9 formalizes cause before court; 10 delivers the public candle-count delay; 11 shows costly aftermath; 12 lands the margin-note ending.

Weaknesses: the overall logic holds, but Chapter 8 is still a little heavier than necessary because Ruan's compromise, Elsbet's internal accounting, and the court-room setup repeat ideas the reader already understands from earlier chapters. The ending pays off the opening by returning to Elsbet's hand and the fair copy. The middle makes the ending more inevitable by converting each proof source into a cost, but Chapter 8 can be sharpened so the private compromise bites faster.
