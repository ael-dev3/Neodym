# Antagonist motive test

## Antagonist

**Ruan Kest**, manor bailiff of Harrowick Ford.

## Clear goal

Secure Nine-Candle Meadow and its ferry landing under a controllable lease before the flood court closes.

## Practical motive

The ferry landing will control spring traffic while the bridge approach is damaged. Traffic means tolls, grain movement, war-road passage, and accounting leverage. Ruan needs clean income and a clean court entry before Lady Merrow's household reviews his accounts.

## Social position

Ruan is not a lord. He is a bailiff: powerful locally, dependent upward, feared downward, and vulnerable if his records look dirty.

## Constraints

- He cannot openly seize chapel records without scandal.
- He cannot make Wren disappear without causing gossip and potential church resistance.
- He needs the steward to enter the decision.
- He needs Father Orlan's seal or at least his silence.
- He needs Elsbet's hand because she writes the fair copy.
- He needs speed because flood court and account review create deadlines.

## Fear

Ruan fears being exposed as a small man who overreached. He also fears that failing to deliver road income will make him disposable to the house he serves.

## Leverage

- Elsbet's household debt.
- Father Orlan's debt and failing eyesight.
- Control of court schedule.
- Wardship threat against Wren.
- Manor candle contract against Matra Voss.
- Public language: he can call a contradiction clerical uncertainty rather than fraud.

## Reason to act now

Floodwater has made the ferry valuable. The court date moved earlier. If Wren's claim survives this hearing, the meadow may be held in ward and Ruan loses the chance to redirect the lease.

## Reason not to use easier methods

- Killing Wren would invite scandal, church action, and suspicion around a valuable meadow.
- Burning the chapel register would damage every local record and threaten court operations.
- Forging the rough register requires physical access he does not have.
- Ignoring the chapel copy makes the manor entry vulnerable.
- Open seizure could provoke rival claimants or Lady Merrow's steward.

## Risk tolerance

High for pressure, threat, omission, debt leverage, and procedural manipulation. Low for public violence or obvious forgery. Ruan wants a lie that looks like paperwork, not a crime that looks like blood.

## Human contradiction

Ruan can believe he is preserving road order and keeping the ford useful during flood season while also stealing a child's inheritance. He can call cruelty administration because the court rewards clean entries over living grief.

## How to keep him from becoming flat

- Show his fear of superiors.
- Show that the ferry genuinely matters to village survival and war traffic.
- Let him be correct that a delayed ferry lease has costs.
- Keep his methods human: pressure, debt, omission, procedure, not cartoon evil.
- Do not let him explain himself in a modern villain speech.
