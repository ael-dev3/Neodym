# Institutional plausibility

## Institution 1: Chapel register and fair copy

### What it does

The chapel records birth, marriage, death, burial candles, tithe promises, and witnessed vows. The rough register is messy but immediate. The quarterly fair copy is clean, sealed, and admissible at manor court.

### Who controls it

Father Orlan Rusk controls the rough register and chapel seal. He delegates copying to Elsbet Vale because she has the steadiest hand and he can no longer trust his eyes for long work.

### Who keeps records

- Rough register: kept by Father Orlan and written partly by Elsbet.
- Fair copy: prepared by Elsbet, checked as much as Father Orlan can manage, sealed with the chapel seal.
- Candle tallies: kept by Matra Voss, the candlewright, and cross-noted in the chapel rough book.

### Where records are kept

- Rough register: locked in the chapel chest under the sacristy shelf.
- Fair copy: carried sealed to the manor court on hearing day.
- Candle tallies: on notched receipt slats in Matra's workroom, not official by themselves.
- Older copies: in the manor chest, controlled by the bailiff and steward.

### Who can access them

- Father Orlan: full chapel access.
- Elsbet: access during copying hours or by explicit instruction.
- Bailiff Ruan: no lawful access to the chapel rough book without priest or court order.
- Ordinary villagers: may hear public readings but cannot handle the register.
- Wren: no formal access.

### Who can alter them

- Father Orlan can correct rough entries in public or with witness.
- Elsbet can write fair copies but cannot lawfully invent corrections.
- Ruan can submit manor claims and pressure wording, but cannot lawfully alter chapel records.
- The steward can reject, delay, or enter the fair copy.

### Punishment for tampering

- False witness fine.
- Loss of chapel copyist post.
- Public penance.
- Debt forfeiture for low-status offenders.
- For a priest: loss of seal right, humiliation, and possible replacement.
- For a bailiff: only if publicly caught; otherwise he hides behind procedure.

### Ordinary interaction

Villagers pay for candles, baptisms, marriage cords, burial fees, and tithe notes. Most cannot read the register, but they know entries matter because court decisions cite them.

### Corrupt exploitation

A bailiff can:

- move the court date forward;
- pressure a weak priest;
- demand a fair copy before witnesses assemble;
- use debt to silence a copyist;
- claim omissions are old clerical mistakes;
- control the manor chest so comparisons become difficult.

### Loopholes

- A rough chapel entry plus material candle proof can force delay.
- Public candle counting can create witness pressure.
- If the fair copy is not yet sealed, Elsbet can refuse to sign it.
- If the steward fears a reversible procedural stain, he may delay rather than enter a disputed record.

### What cannot plausibly happen

- Elsbet cannot casually open the manor chest.
- Ruan cannot openly rewrite the rough chapel book without scandal.
- Wren cannot win full property by simply speaking truth.
- A memory alone cannot overturn a sealed record.
- The Aurelith cannot appear to validate a human register.

## Institution 2: Manor flood court

### What it does

The flood court is an accelerated manor hearing held before spring water cuts roads. It settles rents, ferry leases, wardships, repairs, and emergency road obligations.

### Who controls it

Steward Harl Brey presides in Lady Merrow's name. Bailiff Ruan Kest controls summons, seating, records, and who is called first.

### Records

- Court roll.
- Ferry rent tally.
- Wardship orders.
- Chapel fair copy.
- Repair claims.

### Access

Commoners may attend if summoned, but they speak only when called. Elsbet may be called because her handwriting and witness mark appear on the fair copy.

### Exploitation

Ruan can bury an objection by moving the hearing early, seating allies first, claiming urgency, and presenting the false copy as routine.

### Constraint

The steward cannot ignore a live procedural contradiction in front of witnesses without risking appeal to Lady Merrow's household or the deanery. He need not love justice; he only needs to fear a dirty entry.

## Institution 3: Ferry meadow and wardship

### What it does

Nine-Candle Meadow includes a small cottage, bank, and ferry landing. It is valuable when floodwater closes the stone bridge. If Wren is legitimate, the meadow can be held in ward until she comes of age. If not, it can revert to manor lease.

### Control

- Day to day: Maura Dain's household, now Wren.
- Legal: manor court.
- Practical: whoever controls ferry ropes, landing planks, and road access.

### Why it matters

It is small property with huge seasonal leverage. One ferry landing can decide who moves grain, sheep, messages, and soldiers during flood weeks.

### What cannot happen

A poor child cannot simply defend the meadow. She needs adult witness, procedural delay, and a custodian willing to risk status.

## Institution 4: The Glass Mile taboo

### What it does

The Glass Mile is an old white-stone road or boundary beyond Harrowick Ford. Human banners do not cross it, and cavalry avoids it. No one agrees why.

### Control

No human controls it. Humans control themselves around it through fear, custom, and rumor.

### Use in plot

It shapes roads and ferry value. It does not judge records, punish liars, or explain itself.

### What cannot happen

The Glass Mile cannot solve the inheritance dispute. No Aurelith court, envoy, or miracle appears.
