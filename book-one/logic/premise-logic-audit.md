# Premise logic audit

## Chosen premise

**The Ninth Candle**

A low-born chapel copyist, Elsbet Vale, is preparing the quarterly fair copy of Harrowick Ford's birth, marriage, death, tithe, and candle records when she finds a contradiction: the rough chapel register proves that the dead woman Maura Dain was lawfully married, but the fair copy demanded by the manor bailiff would erase that marriage and make Maura's daughter Wren illegitimate. If the false copy is sealed before the flood court, the ferry meadow passes into the bailiff's control.

## What is the central conflict?

Elsbet must decide whether to sign and seal a false fair copy that protects her family and chapel from reprisal, or expose the contradiction publicly before Wren Dain loses her mother's name, home, and ferry meadow.

## What is the legal/social/economic mechanism behind it?

Harrowick Ford uses a paired record system:

- the chapel keeps rough sacramental and candle records day by day;
- once a quarter, a fair copy is made for the manor court;
- the fair copy is sealed by the priest and witnessed by the copyist;
- the manor steward uses the fair copy to settle wardship, inheritance, dower, and ferry lease disputes;
- if a marriage is absent from the fair copy and no higher sealed copy contradicts it, a child can be treated as base-born or kinless for property purposes.

The disputed property is not a kingdom. It is a narrow meadow and ferry landing. That is small enough for a novella and valuable enough for people to lie.

## Who has authority?

- **Father Orlan Rusk:** chapel priest; controls the rough register, seal, and copyist appointment, but is half-blind and indebted.
- **Bailiff Ruan Kest:** controls manor court scheduling, summons, fines, and access to the manor chest under Lady Merrow's absentee authority.
- **Steward Harl Brey:** presides over the flood court and cares more about clean procedure than justice.
- **Widow Matra Voss:** candlewright and witness to Maura's marriage candle; no formal authority, but has material proof.
- **Elsbet Vale:** no ruling authority, but she has lawful access to the rough register and must witness the fair copy she writes.

## Who has information?

- Elsbet knows the rough register and candle tallies do not match the bailiff's demanded wording.
- Father Orlan remembers enough to be frightened but not enough to trust his eyes.
- Ruan Kest knows the manor copy has already been prepared to omit Maura's marriage.
- Matra Voss knows nine burial candles were bought and that one candle was tied to Maura's married name.
- Wren Dain knows her mother wore a marriage cord but does not know what that can prove.

## Who has physical access?

- Elsbet can access the chapel rough register during appointed copying hours because it is her job.
- Father Orlan controls the chapel key and seal.
- Ruan Kest controls the manor chest, but not the chapel rough book.
- Matra Voss controls her candle moulds, wax tallies, and household receipt slats.
- Wren can reach her mother's cottage and personal objects, but not official records.

## Who has motive?

- Elsbet wants to protect Wren without condemning her own family for false witness or debt forfeiture.
- Ruan wants the ferry meadow transferred before floodwater makes it indispensable to the war road.
- Father Orlan wants to avoid a scandal that could close the chapel and expose his debt.
- Matra wants a dead woman's name kept whole.
- Wren wants not to be sent as a kitchen ward to the same household erasing her.

## Who has opportunity?

Ruan has the opportunity to pressure an early fair copy because the flood court has been moved forward. He cannot safely alter the chapel rough register himself, but he can demand a clean fair copy, threaten debts, and rely on fear to make omissions permanent.

Elsbet has the opportunity to detect the contradiction because she is the lawful copyist. Her access is ordinary, not convenient.

## What is public?

- The flood court date.
- Maura Dain's death.
- The fact that Wren's household is vulnerable.
- The ferry meadow's value after the bridge damage and rising water.
- The chapel's quarterly fair copy custom.

## What is private?

- The mismatch between rough register and manor copy.
- Father Orlan's debt to Ruan.
- Ruan's need to secure ferry income before his own accounts are audited.
- The candlewright's ninth-candle receipt.
- Elsbet's fear that her signature can make a lie lawful.

## What is recorded?

- rough chapel entries: baptisms, marriage cords, deaths, burial candles, tithe notes;
- quarterly fair copy, prepared by Elsbet and sealed by Father Orlan;
- manor court roll, controlled by Ruan and Steward Harl;
- candlewright wax tally and receipt slats;
- ferry rent tally and flood-damage claims.

## What is rumored?

- Maura Dain may have married without enough witnesses.
- Wren's father may have died across the Glass Mile.
- The ferry meadow may be under an old Aurelith-adjacent boundary custom.
- Ruan Kest's son or nephew may need ransom money.
- Father Orlan's eyesight is worse than he admits.

## What is enforceable?

- A sealed fair copy entered at flood court.
- A debt bond against Elsbet's household.
- A wardship order over Wren.
- A ferry lease transfer.
- A fine or public penance for false witness.

## What can be forged, hidden, stolen, misunderstood, delayed, or misread?

- **Forged:** the fair copy, if Elsbet writes omission under pressure.
- **Hidden:** the candle receipt and rough marriage line.
- **Stolen:** Wren's marriage cord token or receipt slat, but only by someone entering her cottage.
- **Misunderstood:** the Glass Mile boundary, which humans use to argue about which road and meadow belong to whom.
- **Delayed:** the flood court hearing, if Elsbet creates enough procedural doubt.
- **Misread:** Father Orlan's weak eyes and tremor let Ruan claim clerical error.

## Why does the protagonist plausibly get involved?

Elsbet is involved before she chooses to be. She is the chapel copyist appointed to prepare the exact fair copy that will decide Wren's status. If she writes the lie, her hand becomes part of the machinery. If she refuses, she risks debt, punishment, and chapel ruin.

## What would a skeptical reader challenge?

1. Why can Elsbet see the record?
2. Why does one omitted marriage matter?
3. Why does Ruan need her instead of forging everything privately?
4. Why does the court care about chapel records?
5. Why would Father Orlan not simply stop it?
6. Why does this happen now?
7. Why does the Aurelith boundary matter without solving anything?

## How does the story answer that challenge?

1. Elsbet sees the record because she is the official chapel copyist.
2. The omitted marriage changes Wren's legitimacy, wardship, and ferry meadow claim.
3. Ruan needs a clean chapel fair copy because the manor copy alone is vulnerable to challenge.
4. The court relies on chapel records because baptisms, marriages, deaths, and burial candles are locally trusted proof.
5. Father Orlan is weak, indebted, half-blind, and frightened of losing the chapel's seal right.
6. The flood court was moved up because rising water makes the ferry landing valuable and urgent.
7. The Glass Mile only shapes routes, taboos, and property arguments; humans still cause and resolve the conflict.

## Audit result

The premise is structurally stronger than the failed levy-bell draft because access, motive, authority, and document custody all begin from the protagonist's ordinary role. The largest remaining risk is making the institution clear without exposition. Chapter 1 must dramatize the record mechanism through pressure, not explanation.
