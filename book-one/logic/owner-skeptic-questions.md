# Owner skeptic questions

## Protagonist access

**Question:** Why can Elsbet access the plot-critical record?

**Current answer:** She is the appointed chapel copyist preparing the quarterly fair copy. The rough register is exactly the material her job requires.

**Weakness:** The appointment must be established fast on page one without sounding like exposition.

**Fix or constraint:** Open Chapter 1 with Elsbet doing the fair-copy work under Father Orlan's instruction while Ruan waits for the sealed copy.

## Official document custody

**Question:** Why is the official record not just sitting around conveniently?

**Current answer:** The rough register is locked in the chapel chest; the manor copy is locked in the manor chest; Elsbet can reach only the chapel record because of her job.

**Weakness:** Readers may not understand rough copy versus fair copy.

**Fix or constraint:** Make the physical difference clear: stained rough book, clean fair sheet, seal tube, court deadline.

## Why one marriage entry matters

**Question:** Why would omitting a marriage change anything serious?

**Current answer:** Wren's legitimacy and wardship affect whether Nine-Candle Meadow stays under her household or reverts to manor lease.

**Weakness:** Inheritance law can feel abstract.

**Fix or constraint:** Put Wren's immediate removal threat on page early: she will be sent to Ruan's cousin's kitchen if the copy is sealed false.

## Why not ask the priest?

**Question:** Why does Father Orlan not solve it?

**Current answer:** He controls the seal but is half-blind, indebted, and afraid the chapel will lose its record right if the contradiction becomes scandal.

**Weakness:** He could look cowardly in a boring way.

**Fix or constraint:** Give him a human reason: losing the seal right would harm every poor household that depends on chapel records, not only him.

## Why not appeal to the lord?

**Question:** Why cannot a higher noble solve it?

**Current answer:** Lady Merrow is absent; the flood court is local; deadlines and roads make distant appeal impossible before the decision.

**Weakness:** Absence can feel convenient.

**Fix or constraint:** Tie absence to established flood-season governance. The steward is present and can delay but not morally rescue.

## Why does Ruan need Elsbet?

**Question:** Why not forge everything himself?

**Current answer:** A manor-only forgery is vulnerable if chapel records contradict it. He needs the chapel fair copy to match or at least not challenge his version.

**Weakness:** The proof chain must be clear.

**Fix or constraint:** Make Ruan explicitly demand omission rather than forged insertion; omission feels plausibly deniable.

## Why does the story happen now?

**Question:** Why today?

**Current answer:** Spring flood damaged the main approach; the flood court moved up; the ferry meadow's value spikes; the quarterly copy is due.

**Weakness:** Too many deadlines can feel stacked.

**Fix or constraint:** Use one visible deadline: the court bell at dusk and rising river marks on the ferry post.

## Why can Elsbet not walk away?

**Question:** Why not say this is not her business?

**Current answer:** Her hand and witness mark will make the fair copy lawful; signing false makes her complicit and punishable. Wren also becomes a ward if she signs.

**Weakness:** Moral obligation alone may not be enough.

**Fix or constraint:** Add practical consequence: Ruan threatens Elsbet's household debt if she refuses, making both choices costly.

## Why cannot Wren solve it?

**Question:** Why does the child need Elsbet?

**Current answer:** Wren lacks legal standing, literacy, adult witness power, and access to records.

**Weakness:** Wren should not become passive.

**Fix or constraint:** Give Wren one necessary action: she brings her mother's marriage cord or refuses to leave the cottage, creating risk.

## Why does the Aurelith layer matter at all?

**Question:** If the story is human, why include the Glass Mile?

**Current answer:** The Glass Mile affects roads, ferry value, and local fear. It is a geographical and cultural pressure, not a plot engine.

**Weakness:** It may feel decorative.

**Fix or constraint:** Tie it to route economics: armies and carts avoid the Glass Mile, so the ferry meadow becomes valuable when floodwater rises.

## Why don't Aurelith intervene?

**Question:** If the boundary is Aurelith, why do they not care?

**Current answer:** Humans do not know whether it is Aurelith, treaty, ruin, taboo, or superstition. It is not evidence in court and receives no explanation.

**Weakness:** Readers may expect payoff.

**Fix or constraint:** The payoff is human: the boundary humbles banners and forces roads around it, but never validates a claim.

## Does the premise pass?

Current answer: **Yes for foundation and outline.** It has legitimate access, clear authority, practical antagonism, and local stakes. It should still be re-audited before Chapter 1 prose.
