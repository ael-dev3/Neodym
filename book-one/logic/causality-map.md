# Causality map

## Setup chain

- Maura Dain dies after winter fever because sickness moves through Harrowick Ford.
- Her daughter Wren becomes legally vulnerable because Maura's husband is dead or absent and Wren is not yet of age.
- Nine-Candle Meadow becomes valuable because floodwater has damaged the main bridge approach and the ferry landing will control spring passage.
- Ruan Kest wants the meadow transferred quickly because his accounts and war-road obligations are due at the flood court.
- Ruan pressures Father Orlan for an early fair copy because the chapel record is the missing piece needed to make the court transfer look clean.
- Father Orlan turns to Elsbet because she is his legitimate copyist and his eyesight is failing.

## Chapter-level cause and effect

- Elsbet begins the fair copy because it is her paid quarterly work.
- She notices a mismatch because the rough register contains Maura's marriage line and a ninth burial candle note, while Ruan's demanded wording omits the marriage.
- This creates immediate danger because signing the fair copy would make Elsbet a witness to the lie.
- Ruan responds by framing the omission as old clerical uncertainty and reminding Elsbet of her household debt.
- Elsbet tries to verify quietly because public accusation without proof would hurt Wren faster.
- Verification forces her to Matra Voss because candle receipt slats can support the rough register.
- Matra hesitates because her candle contract depends on manor favor.
- Wren becomes threatened with wardship because Ruan needs her removed before she can be used as a living claimant.
- Father Orlan is forced to choose because his seal is needed; his fear delays Elsbet, which gives Ruan more time.
- The manor chest remains inaccessible, so Elsbet cannot solve the problem by comparing official copies.
- Elsbet gathers rough register, candle slat, marriage cord, and ferry lease marks because no single piece is enough.
- Ruan escalates by moving the court hour earlier because delay is his enemy.
- The Glass Mile taboo tightens the route because floodwater and old fear make the ferry meadow more important, not because the Aurelith intervene.
- Elsbet chooses public procedural challenge at court because private proof can be buried.
- Public candle counting becomes necessary because the steward can ignore memory but not a visible mismatch among chapel entry, candle tally, and fair copy before summoned witnesses.
- Ruan must either accept delay or deny a concrete count in public.
- His denial exposes his motive because the ninth candle connects Maura's married name to the ferry meadow claim.
- The steward delays the transfer because a dirty court entry threatens future appeal.
- Wren is placed under temporary chapel ward rather than losing the meadow outright.
- Elsbet wins a narrow procedural victory but loses safety because Ruan knows her hand stopped him.

## No random events rule

Every major event must arise from:

- flood timing;
- court procedure;
- record custody;
- debt leverage;
- wardship vulnerability;
- ferry value;
- human fear around the Glass Mile.

If a future scene exists only for mood, cut it or attach it to one of these causes.
