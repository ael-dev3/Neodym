# New canon principles

## Human world first

Edrath is a human world in the ordinary sense that most people living, farming, ruling, praying, stealing, fighting, and dying inside it are human. Human institutions are not puppet machinery for the Aurelith. They are old, proud, stupid, holy, greedy, brave, and contradictory in their own right.

Human society includes:

- kingdoms and border marches
- noble houses and local dynasties
- peasant villages and market towns
- serfs, freedmen, guilds, monasteries, hedge knights, bailiffs, priests, bandits, smugglers, widows, fools, healers, and war children
- human courts, human taxes, human cruelty, human mercy
- local gods, saint cults, heresies, harvest rites, funerals, oaths, songs, and inherited lies

## Aurelith distance

The Aurelith are not daily administrators of human life. They are a distant elder civilization whose works mark the world unevenly.

They may appear through:

- a roadless territory no army crosses
- an impossible tower on a horizon
- a treaty older than the kingdom enforcing it
- a boundary stone nobody dares move
- an artifact in a noble vault
- a strange light seen from fields once or twice a year
- an envoy whose silence changes a war
- a religious argument about what humans once saw

They should not appear as routine village clerks, teachers, correction officers, or census keepers.

## Human interpretations are not canon facts

Humans misread the Aurelith constantly. Priests call them angels, devils, elder cousins, soulless immortals, or tests sent by the gods. Noble houses claim old favor they cannot prove. Peasants pass down warnings whose reasons are gone.

A human explanation of Aurelith behavior is usually a human explanation, not truth.

## Human suffering has human authors

Most human suffering should come from humans: debt, law, war, hunger, inheritance, class, gender expectations, border hatred, religious fear, bad kings, cruel bailiffs, cowardice, greed, and ordinary panic.

Aurelith presence may shape the terrain of a conflict, but it should rarely be the direct cause.

## Neodym

Neodym remains rare and strange. It is tied to Aurelith works, old ruins, impossible craft, and some forms of memory or oath. It should not become a daily human resource or the explanation for every custom.

For Book One, Neodym should stay mostly off page.

## Book One rule

Book One must read as a human tale. It may happen near an Aurelith boundary. It may contain fear of something older than human kingdoms. It must not become a manual for elf law.
