# Book One replacement plan

## Chosen direction

Use Low Wicker, but humanize it.

The new first novella is a small human border story set in a sheep valley near an old Aurelith boundary. The conflict is not an elf-law case. It is a human levy, a burned tithe barn, a false accusation, and a letter that can save a boy from being taken into a lord's war.

## Working title

Primary working title: **The Field Where No Banners Go**

Other possible titles:

1. The Field Where No Banners Go
2. The Ash Road Letter
3. The Boy on the Levy Roll
4. No Banner at Starvel Field
5. The Road Around the White Stones

## One-sentence pitch

A village letter-reader must carry proof of a lord's false war levy through disputed human country before a boy she loves is taken, while every road bends around an Aurelith boundary no army dares cross.

## Why this is stronger

- The story starts with human stakes: a levy roll, a child at risk, a burned barn, a hidden letter.
- Nera has an immediate desire on page one: keep Pell off the war list and get proof to someone who can stop the levy.
- Pell matters as a person in danger, not as a magical trigger.
- The antagonist is human greed and war politics.
- The Aurelith layer is a forbidden field and a set of old human taboos, not an active legal machine.
- The story can work with no elf on page.

## Core cast

- Nera Brindle: Low-born village letter-reader, toll scribe, and baker's assistant. She survives by making words fit what powerful people want to see. Her flaw is practical cowardice dressed as care.
- Pell Quick: Thirteen-year-old orphan, errand runner, unofficial younger brother to Nera. Fast, hungry, funny when safe, quiet when he knows adults are lying.
- Osa Fen: Owner of the Split Loaf. She loves Nera and Pell, but she knows hunger makes brave talk expensive.
- Osric Venn: Bailiff's man and local debt-holder. He helps turn a burned barn into a levy because war can make his house rich.
- Joryn Hale: Poor local guard with a decent heart and a bad oath. He knows the levy is dirty but fears what open refusal costs.
- Sister Auma: Chapel keeper who knows old border customs and which roads armies avoid.
- Bruk of the Low Cairns: Stonemender and road guide. He can read old roadbeds, burn marks, and boundary stones better than the men who own them.

## Aurelith use

Book One uses contact Levels 0 through 2.

- Starvel Field is not a court. It is a boundary.
- White stones mark ground where banners are lowered and horses baulk.
- Armies avoid the field because every old campaign that crossed it ended strangely, but stories disagree about how.
- No Aurelith appears to explain or judge.
- Human characters exploit the taboo, fear it, misread it, and route power around it.

## Ending promise

Nera gets one human-sized victory. Pell is spared the levy. The false fire is exposed enough to blunt one lord's claim. Low Wicker keeps some grain and loses some safety. No Aurelith intervenes. Starvel Field remains what it was: a silent edge in a human world.
