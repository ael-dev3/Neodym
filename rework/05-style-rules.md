# Style rules after the thin-veil rework

## Prose

Open with pressure. Introduce characters by what they are trying to do, hide, refuse, or survive.

Good scene materials:

- levy rolls
- bread ovens
- muddy roads
- burned barns
- ransom law
- sheep disease
- bad boots
- oaths
- debt marks
- border stones
- rain
- ash
- iron keys
- old songs
- chapel wax
- wet wool
- human banners

Avoid decorative market texture unless it changes the scene.

## Aurelith restraint

Use Aurelith elements like weather, cliffs, old plague ground, or a mountain no one climbs. They should alter human choices without becoming the obvious plot machine.

Avoid:

- elf courts explaining themselves
- village-level elf bureaucracy
- direct elf policing of ordinary human life
- routine magical identity systems
- technical explanations
- human characters who know too much about Aurelith motives

## Human drama

Make humans responsible for the story.

Useful human pressures:

- debt
- hunger
- war levy
- inheritance
- marriage bargain
- rival houses
- cowardice
- protection money
- land claims
- religious fear
- class contempt
- a lie everyone benefits from until someone gets hurt

## First chapter rule

On page one, answer these through action:

- Who is the protagonist?
- What do they want today?
- What interrupts them?
- What happens if they fail?
- Why should the reader care?
- What kind of story is this?

If a detail does not sharpen those answers, cut it or move it later.
