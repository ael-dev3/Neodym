# Visual Style Guide

## Core visual thesis

Neodym should look like a dark epic fantasy world where sacred empire, rare-earth mineral mysticism, archive mythology, and beautiful oppression have grown together for centuries. The Aurelith world is elegant, cold, silver, predictive, and inhumanly composed. Human life is warm, irregular, crowded, smoky, musical, and emotionally immediate.

## Primary cover reference

Use [`assets/neodym-cover.png`](../assets/neodym-cover.png) as the main visual anchor for future covers, repo imagery, concept prompts, and mood boards.

Key motifs to preserve:

- A high-contrast split between a cold, silver Aurelith city above and a hot, smoky human rebellion below.
- Monumental crystal towers, suspended bridges, moons, hanging Neodym shards, and an ornamental silver sigil.
- Immortal elf-like figures staged with ritual stillness, distance, and control.
- Human rebels seen from below and behind, moving through ash, firelight, banners, crowds, and ruined streets.
- Typography and ornament that feel carved from silver, old stone, and sacred geometry rather than modern branding.

The reference image should guide composition and atmosphere, not freeze every design choice. New art can vary characters, locations, scale, and narrative moment as long as it keeps the same tension: beautiful immortal order above, mortal heat and danger below.

## Main color palette

- **Neodym silver-blue:** luminous blue-silver used for sacred metal, memory light, archive seams, identity seals.
- **Moon ivory:** Aurelith skin, robes, bridges, and ritual stone.
- **Black glass:** mirrors, court floors, archive pools, prophecy surfaces.
- **Deep obsidian:** mountains, Dravyr relics, vault foundations.
- **Blood red:** human rebellion, Bloodsong, red thread, candle wax, sacrifice.
- **Ash white:** Eirrem, erased memory, funerary marks.
- **Warm clay and bread gold:** human homes, bakeries, hearths, worker districts.

## Secondary palette

- Rust orange for mines and worker tools.
- Verdigris green for old copper, Thallor moss, and drowned ruins.
- Pearl black and ink blue for Nacren sea culture.
- Storm gray and bone tan for Vharakul borderlands.
- Ember gold for Dravyr heartfire.
- Lichen amber for Cairnkin brightstone sickness.

## Materials

Neodym, moon-glass, black mirror stone, crystal, ivory, ash, bone, rusted iron, basalt, lichen, worn linen, bread, wax, red thread, salt, shell, coral, obsidian, old wood, clay, copper, fungus, living root, volcanic glass.

## Lighting motifs

- Aurelith: cold moonlight, reflected light, symmetrical halos, silver mist, indirect glow from archive crystals.
- Humans: candlelight, hearth glow, smoky morning, festival lanterns, hot red song-light.
- Mines: blue ore glow, amber lamps, rust fog, black pressure shadows.
- Sea: moonlit water, pearl bioluminescence, storm flashes through glass waves.
- Forbidden magic: red thread-light, breath vapor, candle flames bending toward voices.

## Architecture rules

- **Aurelith:** crescent forms, suspended bridges, impossible symmetry, towers like frozen roots, mirror balconies, quiet plazas, thin silver ribs, monumental but weightless.
- **Human:** dense, repaired, hand-built, crooked lanes, cloth awnings, clay brick, wood beams, visible domestic life, hidden shrines.
- **Cairnkin:** basalt vaults, low arches, pressure doors, tool shrines, glowing lichen, carved air channels.
- **Vharakul:** bone towers, hide tents, storm poles, grassland forts, trophy poetry carved into horn and wood.
- **Thallor:** root bridges, fungal domes, drowned libraries, spore lanterns, living walls.
- **Nacren:** coral courts, shell gates, suspended kelp silk, pearl-dark stone, submerged moon windows.
- **Dravyr:** volcanic temples, chain halos, furnace sanctuaries, obsidian scales as architecture.

## Clothing rules

- Aurelith clothing should be layered, precise, ritualized, pale, and threaded with Neodym. It should look grown or folded by geometry, not tailored for comfort.
- Human clothing should be mended, practical, layered, textured, warm-toned, with hidden red thread or coded embroidery.
- Collaborators wear human fabrics cut to Aurelith symmetry; they look elegant but constrained.
- Rebels hide symbols inside everyday garments rather than wearing obvious uniforms.
- Other races must show material culture: Cairnkin respirator veils, Vharakul oath scars, Nacren kelp silk, Thallor moss robes, Caltrow patchwork bells.

## Symbolic shapes

- Aurelith: crescent, mirror oval, vertical silver thread, perfect circle, nine-point star.
- Human rebellion: broken circle, ember, red knot, burnt grain stalk, open hand.
- Neodym: thin veins, magnetic halos, blue-silver crystalline facets.
- Memory: bottles, moths, threads, mirrors, layered faces, sealed eyes.
- Prediction: orreries, suspended rings, descending silver lines, black reflective pools.
- Freedom: dangerous flame, crooked road, bird made of bread and wire.

## Elf / Neodym visual language

Aurelith should look ancient, beautiful, quietly oppressive, and slightly post-human without sci-fi cues. Their presence should imply order, prediction, and emotional restraint. Neodym should never look like ordinary steel; it glows with trapped memory, bends dust and filings around it, and creates subtle halos around edges.

## Human visual language

Human spaces should feel alive: imperfect faces, wrinkles, sweat, crowded rooms, family objects, smoke, hand lettering, patched cloth, food, children, old songs. Even in oppression, human warmth should remain visually powerful.

## Magic visual language

- Aurelith magic is geometric, silver, quiet, reflective, and precise.
- Human Bloodsong is red, breath-based, candlelit, imperfect, embodied, and costly.
- Thallor magic is spore-light and root memory.
- Nacren magic is tide reflection, salt knots, and moonlit dissolution.
- Caltrow magic is chalk, bells, misaligned shadows, and playful asymmetry.

## Oppression visual language

Oppression should look useful, beautiful, and intimate: bread ledgers, child measuring rods, silver seals cutting skin, mirror wells in clean squares, calm guards at warm public bakeries, legal mercy masks, public order that feels almost holy.

## Rebellion visual language

Rebellion should look hidden, handmade, fragile, and hot: red thread under cuffs, burnt grain marks, candle codes, quilt maps, ash bowls, bread stamps, wrong notes, cellar chapels, smuggled memory vials.

## Sacred object design rules

Sacred objects should be old, used, and symbolically layered. Avoid clean fantasy props. Add scratches, oil, ash, fingerprints, repaired cracks, ritual wrapping, and signs of conflicting ownership.

## Map design rules

Maps may be in-world artifacts or clean reference maps. Use parchment, etched silver, stitched cloth, crystal projection, rebel charcoal, shell inlay, basalt carving, or archive vellum. Official maps should be symmetrical and censoring. Rebel maps should be practical, annotated, and partial.

## Technology-disguised-as-magic rules

Surveillance, identity, memory storage, prediction, and civic infrastructure must be visualized through mirrors, seals, ledgers, crystal groves, moon vaults, living spells, oracle rings, census hymns, and archive temples. Avoid modern interfaces, screens, wires, robots, or plastic surfaces.

## What to avoid

Avoid generic sci-fi, modern screens, obvious robots, futuristic guns, plastic armor, Tolkien-copy elves, generic D&D costumes, random neon cityscapes, anime style unless requested, cheerful cartoon tone, low-detail fantasy tropes, overly clean poster art. Also avoid copying recognizable designs, heraldry, costumes, architecture, or species traits from existing fantasy franchises.

## Consistency rules

- Use **Aurelith** for the elf-like race and **Quiet Concord** for their ruling order.
- Use **Neodym** as the sacred blue-silver metal.
- Use **Edrath** for the world/continent.
- Use **Emberwake** for the main human-led resistance.
- Use **Mirror Choir** for surveillance and **Name-Irons** for identity seals.
- Keep Aurelith spaces cold and symmetrical; human spaces warm and asymmetrical.
- Red thread, burnt grain, and candlelight belong to rebellion.
- Crescent mirrors, silver threads, and black orreries belong to the Concord.
