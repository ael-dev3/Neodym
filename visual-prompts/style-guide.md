# Visual prompt style guide

## Direction

Neodym should look like a human high-fantasy world with an older civilization at the edge of sight. Human scenes come first: roads, rain, banners, bread, ash, wool, fields, chapels, knives, debt ledgers, border stones, and tired faces.

Aurelith elements should be rare and quiet: impossible white stones, a distant tower, blue-white light without flame, a road no horse will take, a structure that ignores weather.

## Book One palette

- mud brown
- wet wool gray
- barley gold
- ash black
- iron dark
- chapel wax yellow
- banner red or boar bronze
- rare blue-white elder light

## Composition rules

- Put humans in the foreground.
- Keep Aurelith structures distant or partially obscured.
- Make landscapes practical: roads need carts, gates, ditches, sheep, mills, barns, and tracks.
- Avoid clean science-fiction surfaces.
- Avoid making every costume or object elf-themed.

## Negative prompt baseline

Avoid modern technology, screens, lasers, robots, plastic armor, neon cyberpunk, spaceship aesthetics, generic roleplaying-game armor, cheerful cartoon tone, obvious franchise references, and overdesigned elf bureaucracy.
